\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Message Queuing Telemetry Transport (MQTT) protocol carries the publish--subscribe traffic of many Internet of Things (IoT) deployments, and its brokers are exposed to denial-of-service (DoS) and distributed DoS (DDoS) attacks that abuse the protocol itself. The DoS/DDoS-MQTT-IoT dataset, captured on a physical testbed, records normal MQTT traffic together with ten such attack scenarios~\cite{alatram2023}. Intrusion detection systems (IDSs) based on machine learning learn these attacks from labeled traffic, so their quality depends on how much representative traffic of each attack they see. In practice that traffic is spread over many gateways that cannot pool it. Federated learning (FL) trains a shared model while the data stay local: a server distributes a global model and aggregates the clients' updates over several rounds~\cite{mcmahan2017}. Keeping data local, however, does not remove the privacy risk, because shared gradients can reveal training records~\cite{zhu2019}, and privacy-preserving FL for IoT intrusion detection remains an active field~\cite{vyas2024}.

Two difficulties combine in this setting. First, rare attack classes are scattered unevenly across gateways (non-IID data): a gateway may hold a handful of packets of a rare attack, or none, which biases any local detector toward the majority classes. Second, the two standard protections answer different threats. Secure aggregation (SecAgg) hides each client's update from the server and reveals only their sum~\cite{bonawitz2017, bell2020}, but it says nothing about what the sum, the trained model or its synthetic data reveal. Differential privacy (DP) bounds that disclosure for any single record~\cite{dwork2014}, at a cost in utility. Federated generative models have been proposed to synthesize the missing attack traffic~\cite{deng2025, hamouda2024, yao2024, shettar2026}. These are deep networks: they exchange full models over tens of rounds and, when trained with DP stochastic gradient descent (DP-SGD)~\cite{abadi2016dpsgd}, spread the privacy noise over thousands of optimization steps.

Marginal-based synthesis takes another route: it measures low-order count tables with DP noise and samples records from a graphical model fitted to them~\cite{zhang2017privbayes, mckenna2021mst, mckenna2024aim}. Count tables are sums over records, which is exactly what secure aggregation computes. Discrete noise such as the Skellam distribution can therefore be split across clients so that the server sees only the noisy sum~\cite{kairouz2021, agarwal2021}. Federated marginal synthesizers already exist~\cite{maddock2024, pentyala2024}, but they have been evaluated on general tabular benchmarks rather than on downstream detection of rare attacks in IoT traffic, and not against a federated deep generator under the same protections.

This paper studies that comparison on MQTT traffic with PP-FedData, a privacy-preserving federated data generation framework. It addresses four research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} How much IDS utility does federated synthetic data retain when raw traffic cannot be pooled, and does it add utility when real data are available?
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} Under the same $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ budget, how does a federated marginal generator compare with a federated CVAE trained with DP-SGD in utility, rare-class recall and communication?
    \item \textbf{RQ3:} What does each protection (DP, SecAgg$^+$, both) cost, and what does it protect, as a function of the protected unit (packet, TCP stream, capture) and of the number of honest gateways?
    \item \textbf{RQ4:} Which configuration should a deployment choose given its trust, privacy and bandwidth requirements?
\end{itemize}

The contributions are the following. We do not claim a new synthesis algorithm: FedDP-Marginal adapts tree-structured marginal synthesis~\cite{chow1968, mckenna2021mst}, and federated marginal synthesis has been studied before~\cite{maddock2024, pentyala2024}. What we add is the combination and the evidence.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{FedDP-Marginal for IoT-MQTT traffic.} A three-release protocol in which gateways add shares of integer Skellam noise to their count tables and sum them with a real SecAgg$^+$ implementation in Flower; the privacy loss is accounted exactly and reported for every number of honest gateways, with options for a threshold of honest gateways, local DP, and stream- or capture-level protection.
    \item \textbf{A controlled benchmark against a federated CVAE.} Both generators run on the same non-IID partitions, privacy budgets, secure aggregation and evaluation, which separates the cost of DP, the cost of SecAgg$^+$ and the effect of the generator family.
    \item \textbf{A requirement-driven configuration guide.} A transparent rule filters the measured configurations by trust, privacy budget, bandwidth and attack priority and selects from their Pareto front.
\end{enumerate}
