\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
PP-FedData generates synthetic IoT-MQTT traffic across gateways that cannot share raw packets; this paper compared two federated generators under the same partitions, privacy budgets and secure aggregation. FedDP-Marginal, which releases three sums of noisy count tables with Skellam noise split across gateways and summed by SecAgg$^+$, kept 0.389--0.429 test macro-F1 at $\varepsilon = 1$--10, against 0.227--0.289 for a federated CVAE trained with DP-SGD, while sending less than 1\,MB per run. Under the CVAE's own trust model, with the noise added at each gateway, it could not be separated from the CVAE at $\varepsilon = 1$ and led at $\varepsilon = 5$ and 10. Secure aggregation cost little and no utility; DP cost FedDP-Marginal 0.02--0.06 macro-F1 and lowered the AUC of an attack on its released tables from 0.560 to 0.514--0.531, although the leakage was small in both cases. Synthetic data did not improve an IDS whose real data could be pooled, synthetic packets remained distinguishable from real ones, and the guarantee is per packet unless a stream-level unit is chosen. The framework is therefore a way to train or complete an IDS where traffic must stay local, and its requirement-driven rule tells a deployment which protection to choose and what it will cost.

Future work will re-extract the normal traffic with the dataset authors' export settings, evaluate other IoT datasets, compare against FLAIM, AIM and a CVAE with distributed noise, estimate the preprocessing statistics and select hyperparameters privately, and measure the protocols on physical gateways over a real network.
