\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper presented PP-FedData, a framework for generating synthetic IoT-MQTT traffic across gateways that cannot share raw packets, and compared two federated generators under the same partitions, privacy budgets and secure aggregation. FedDP-Marginal, which releases three sums of noisy count tables with Skellam noise split across gateways and summed by SecAgg$^+$, kept 0.389--0.429 test macro-F1 at $\varepsilon = 1$--10, against 0.227--0.289 for a federated CVAE trained with DP-SGD, while sending less than 1\,MB per run. Secure aggregation cost little and no utility; DP cost FedDP-Marginal 0.02--0.06 macro-F1 and measurably reduced what an attack on its released tables could detect. The results also bound the claim. Synthetic data did not improve an IDS whose real data could be pooled, synthetic packets remained distinguishable from real ones, and the guarantee is per packet unless a stream-level unit is chosen. The framework is therefore a way to train or complete an IDS where traffic must stay local, and its requirement-driven rule tells a deployment which protection to choose and what it will cost.

Future work will re-extract the normal traffic with the dataset authors' export settings, evaluate other IoT datasets and protocols, compare against FLAIM and AIM directly, account for hyperparameter selection privately, and measure the protocols on physical gateways over a real network.
