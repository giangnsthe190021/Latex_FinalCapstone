\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

This thesis studied how several MQTT gateways can jointly produce data for training an intrusion detection system without releasing their raw packets, and what each protection costs. It built PP-FedData, a framework evaluated on the DoS/DDoS-MQTT-IoT dataset under a split by capture (by block of the capture for the four classes recorded in a single capture), with five leakage checks, a locked test set and a paired-bootstrap decision rule. Two generator routes were compared under the same federation: FedDP-Marginal, which releases noisy count tables in three rounds and samples from a Chow--Liu tree, and a federated conditional variational autoencoder trained with DP-SGD.

Three conclusions stand out.

First, for protected generation on this data and protocol, the tested marginal pipelines are the better choice. At the same privacy budget, with the noise split over the gateways, they keep a higher macro-F1 and a higher rare-class recall than the CVAE route chosen on validation, by $+0.158$ to $+0.191$ and $+0.155$ to $+0.169$ (with local noise, by $+0.092$ to $+0.143$ and $+0.049$ to $+0.147$). These three matched runs send 0.48 to 0.53\,MB in three rounds against 13.6 to 72.4\,MB over many model rounds. The comparison is between implemented pipelines and does not isolate the architecture alone. The releases are sums, so the noise can be split across gateways through secure aggregation, which sums the integer counts exactly.

Second, generated data have a specific place. When real data can be pooled, they offer no advantage over the tested class-weight and SMOTE baselines, which are preferable there. They are valuable where pooling is impossible, with a synthetic-only macro-F1 of 0.366 to 0.433 at $\varepsilon$ from 1 to 10 against 0.448 for real data only. At $\varepsilon = 5$ and $\varepsilon = 10$ this is also ahead of an MLP trained directly by DP-FedSGD under the same distributed DP, and at $\varepsilon = 1$ the rule detects no difference. Without noise, the direct MLP has the higher macro-F1 ($+0.008$) and the lower rare-class recall ($-0.040$), so the answer depends on the metric. Synthetic rows also help a gateway that lacks some classes: added to its own rows, they raise its macro-F1 by about 0.04 over local training alone, and training on synthetic rows alone is a competitive alternative.

Third, a privacy claim is meaningful only together with its unit and its trust assumption. Protecting a TCP stream is affordable, at 0.03 to 0.05 of macro-F1, and protecting a whole capture is not usable on this data. With one honest gateway out of five, an $\varepsilon$ of 5 becomes 14.2. A configuration should therefore be selected by requirement. The study proposes a rule that does so from measured values for eleven deployment scenarios; the rule rests on assumed requirements and thresholds and has not been tested in a deployment.

These conclusions apply to one dataset, packet-level records, five gateways simulated on one machine, and three seeds, and they rest on the trust assumptions of Section~\ref{subsec:threat_model}. The reported $\varepsilon$ values describe the accounted mechanisms under secret noise and fixed public preprocessing; they exclude private selection, and the deterministic research simulation is not an operational private release. Marginal-based federated synthesis is not new, and this work claims no algorithmic novelty. Its contributions are the distributed accounting of integer noise summed exactly through a real secure-aggregation implementation, the controlled comparison of the two generator families and of the directly trained detector, the evidence on when synthetic data are worth using, and the evidence on evaluation practice (Section~\ref{sec:contribution}); the configuration guide is a proposal built on them.

\section{Future Work}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stronger generators under the same conditions.} Run FLAIM, AIM and other marginal mechanisms, together with DP-CTGAN or diffusion-based models, under the same federation, DP accounting and SecAgg$^+$, so that FedDP-Marginal can be ranked against them.
    \item \textbf{Richer structure.} Study higher-order or class-specific graphical structures with private selection, to raise utility beyond the current plateau at moderate budgets.
    \item \textbf{Session-level modelling.} Generate sequences of packets or flow-level records, so that temporal attack patterns are represented and capture-level protection becomes feasible.
    \item \textbf{An operational release.} Draw the noise of every gateway from a cryptographically secure generator, remove the diagnostic files from storage the server can read, and audit the implementation for side channels, so that a release can be made outside the research simulation.
    \item \textbf{Ablations of the tree structure.} Compare the plug-in, Miller--Madow and normalised MI for the Chow--Liu tree, and measure the stability of its edges across partition seeds, which this study proposed but did not run.
    \item \textbf{Private preprocessing and selection.} Compute normalisation and category statistics securely, or fix them in advance, and add a private selection step so that the tuning falls inside the privacy accounting.
    \item \textbf{Real deployment measurements.} Run the protocol on real gateways over constrained links such as NB-IoT or LoRa, to measure latency, packet loss, dropouts and energy use.
    \item \textbf{Other datasets and larger federations.} Repeat the study on other MQTT and IoT traffic, with a wider range of $K$ and with client dropout, so that the effect of $t$ can be studied beyond $K = 5$.
    \item \textbf{Malicious participants.} Extend the threat model to poisoning and Byzantine behaviour, including robust aggregation compatible with distributed noise.
    \item \textbf{Group-level attacks.} Develop membership and attribute attacks at the level of a TCP stream or capture, to test what the group bounds protect in practice.
    \item \textbf{Hybrid detection.} Combine a privately trained detector with local augmentation by generated data, and measure whether the two routes complement each other on the rare classes.
\end{itemize}
