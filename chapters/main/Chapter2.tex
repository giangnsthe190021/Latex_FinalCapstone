\section{Related Work and Research Gaps}\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Privacy-Preserving Mechanisms in Federated Learning}

\subsubsection{Landscape and Scope of Research}
The integration of Federated Learning (FL) into Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) for IoT environments has resolved raw data centralization, yet the model weight sharing process itself still harbors data leakage risks~\cite{vyas2024}. Current studies classify Privacy-Preserving Federated Learning (PPFL) techniques into five main categories: Differential Privacy (DP), Homomorphic Encryption (HE), Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMPC), Trusted Execution Environments (TEE), and Blockchain~\cite{vyas2024}. Although TEEs provide a secure execution environment and Blockchain ensures transparency, they require specialized hardware or complex consensus mechanisms unsuitable for the constrained resources of edge IoT devices~\cite{manzano2025}. Therefore, this study implements DP and SMPC-based secure aggregation (SecAgg$^+$); HE is reviewed as an alternative but not implemented due to its prohibitive computational cost on constrained devices~\cite{nakayiza2026}.

\subsubsection{Trusted Execution Environments (TEE)}
The Trusted Execution Environment (TEE) provides an isolated and secure hardware computing enclave for processing sensitive telemetry, effectively preserving confidentiality. However, this technology depends heavily on specialized hardware security modules. This strict hardware requirement makes TEE cumbersome and unsuitable for ubiquitous deployment across heterogeneous IoT networks comprising massively resource-constrained devices~\cite{vyas2024}.

\subsubsection{Blockchain-Based Mechanisms}
Blockchain preserves data privacy through decentralized ledgers and cryptographic consensus that ensure tamper-resistance and eliminate intermediaries. However, these architectures suffer from fatal performance weaknesses due to low throughput, high latency, and expensive verification costs stemming from continuously growing immutable storage. These latency and storage overheads make blockchain impractical for lightweight, multi-device IoT environments that demand rapid detection response times~\cite{vyas2024}.

\subsubsection{Homomorphic Encryption (HE)}
HE enables arithmetic operations directly on encrypted ciphertexts, ensuring model updates from clients remain confidential prior to server aggregation without decryption~\cite{nakayiza2026}. However, the computational complexity and communication bandwidth expansion of HE algorithms are exceptionally heavy, drastically increasing system latency~\cite{nakayiza2026}. This renders HE unfeasible for edge IDS requiring real-time response under tight resource constraints~\cite{nakayiza2026}.

\subsubsection{Differential Privacy (DP)}
Unlike the heavy overhead of HE, Differential Privacy (DP) offers high scalability. The core principle of DP is to mask the contribution of any individual record by injecting calibrated random perturbation into local updates before transmission~\cite{ruzafa2022}. DP is exceptionally lightweight, incurs negligible transmission overhead, and scales easily to large client cohorts~\cite{ruzafa2022}. The trade-off is that noise injection degrades model parameter precision, necessitating careful calibration of the privacy budget $\varepsilon$~\cite{ruzafa2022}.

\subsubsection{Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMPC/SecAgg)}
Conversely, Secure Aggregation (SecAgg) protocols based on SMPC resolve the accuracy degradation of DP. SMPC prevents the server from inspecting individual client updates by ensuring that only the aggregated sum is revealed~\cite{bessaa2026}. SecAgg introduces zero statistical perturbation; under the fixed-point quantization utilized in SecAgg$^+$, the aggregate differs from the plain sum only by bounded quantization error~\cite{bell2020}. Although the original SecAgg protocol suffered from multi-round interaction bottlenecks scaling quadratically with client counts~\cite{bonawitz2017}, the SecAgg$^+$ protocol resolves these issues by replacing complete communication graphs with $k$-regular graphs, achieving polylogarithmic communication and computational complexity~\cite{bell2020}.

\subsubsection{Hybrid Privacy-Preserving Systems}
Gutti et~al.~\cite{gutti2024} (combining DP and SecAgg via the HADA algorithm) demonstrated that pairing DP (to prevent post-aggregation inference) with SecAgg (to secure transmission channels) is a promising paradigm. However, stacking these security layers introduces substantial hyperparameter tuning and coordination complexity~\cite{gutti2024}. Evaluating independent and joint performance trade-offs under realistic edge conditions is therefore essential before deploying hybrid pipelines.

\subsubsection{Distributed Differential Privacy}
Distributed DP combines the strengths of both paradigms: each client adds only a fraction of the required noise, and secure aggregation reveals only the collective sum containing the full calibrated perturbation~\cite{kairouz2021, agarwal2021}. Because secure aggregation operates over modular integer lattices, discrete noise distributions are preferred; the sum of independent Skellam variables remains Skellam, permitting exact privacy accounting after modular aggregation~\cite{agarwal2021}. Distributed DP is particularly well-suited for single-sum releases such as contingency count tables.

\subsection{Generative Data Augmentation for Intrusion Detection}

\subsubsection{Taxonomy and Core Concepts}
Generative AI paradigms in IoT-IDS learn the underlying multivariate probability distributions of network telemetry to automatically synthesize realistic traffic samples whose structural and statistical attributes align with genuine traffic~\cite{deng2025}.

\noindent\textbf{Core problems addressed by generative augmentation:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mitigating Extreme Class Imbalance:} Real-world IoT traffic exhibits severe class skewness, where over 90--99\% of traffic comprises benign activities, while rare DoS/DDoS attacks constitute negligible fractions. This deficit biases downstream classifiers toward majority classes, generating unacceptable false negatives on stealthy threats~\cite{deng2025}.
    \item \textbf{Surpassing Interpolation-Based Oversampling:} Traditional oversampling algorithms such as SMOTE~\cite{chawla2002} perform linear interpolation within localized convex hulls. Deep generative models capture global distributions and explore under-represented regions of feature space to synthesize zero-day attack variants. Furthermore, SMOTE requires centralized access to neighboring minority instances, making it inapplicable to federated environments where individual edge gateways possess few or no attack records~\cite{chawla2002}.
    \item \textbf{Data Augmentation and Restructuring:} Systematic surveys show that up to 89\% of studies leverage generative models for data augmentation and class rebalancing, improving classification generalization without centralizing raw traffic~\cite{deng2025}.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Primary taxonomies of generative architectures in IDS:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs):} Structured around an adversarial minimax game between a Generator and a Discriminator. Modeling sharp distributions makes GANs the predominant architecture (58\%) in generative IoT-IDS~\cite{deng2025}.
    \item \textbf{Variational Autoencoders (VAEs):} Encoders map high-dimensional traffic into continuous latent manifolds, while Decoders reconstruct inputs. VAEs enforce structured prior distributions, permitting stable sampling without minimax instabilities~\cite{deng2025}.
    \item \textbf{Transformer-Based Models:} Multi-head self-attention mechanisms model complex sequential dependencies across packet streams~\cite{deng2025}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Critical Analysis of Generative Approaches}
Existing literature reveals noticeable bottlenecks when deploying generative models in decentralized edge settings~\cite{deng2025}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tabular Diffusion (Tab-DDPM):} Manzano et~al.~\cite{manzano2025} integrated tabular diffusion on the BoT-IoT benchmark. While Tab-DDPM achieves high fidelity, iterative denoising over hundreds of reverse steps incurs ~32 seconds per sample on edge nodes (1 CPU core, 2\,GB RAM), which is infeasible for line-rate inspection.
    \item \textbf{Policy-Guided GANs:} Yang et~al.~\cite{yang2025} employed RL-guided GANs for DDoS augmentation. However, the multi-component architecture requires 2.726 seconds per batch and 19.6\,GB of GPU memory on KDDcup99, without isolating standalone edge inference latency.
    \item \textbf{Adversarial Instability:} Deng et~al.~\cite{deng2025} observed that GAN training is prone to mode collapse, wasting node resources by repeatedly generating duplicate records.
    \item \textbf{Resampling Baselines (Bootstrapping):} Jacob and Sultana~\cite{jacob2026} used bootstrapping to balance BoT-IoT and CSE-CIC-IDS2018 datasets. While lightweight, duplication does not expand feature diversity, increasing overfitting risks.
    \item \textbf{Centralized Cloud VAE-GAN:} Anandhi and Srikanth~\cite{anandhi2026} synthesized 51,200 Low-Rate Stealth Attack samples in the cloud, raising recall from 62\% to 98\%. However, cloud synthesis violates federated privacy principles, and compressing models below 6 bits degraded detection sensitivity.
    \item \textbf{Lightweight Edge GANs (TinyGAN):} Durga et~al.~\cite{durga2026} deployed TinyGAN for IoMT malware detection. However, all classes below 0.5\% prevalence were discarded, avoiding the fundamental minority imbalance challenge.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Privacy-Preserving Generative Defenses and Identified Research Gaps}

\subsubsection{Privacy-Utility Dilemma in Edge Generative IDS}
Federated IDS on edge networks must protect client telemetry while coping with severe minority-class imbalance and non-IID traffic distributions. Differential privacy and cryptographic protocols bound gradient inversion and membership inference risks~\cite{cunhaneto2023} but do not correct class skew. Augmenting under-represented attack classes with federated generative models mitigates this imbalance, yet generative architectures may memorize training records and expose sensitive network traces through parameter exchanges or synthesized flows~\cite{cunhaneto2023}. Edge defenses must therefore limit the utility loss of synthetic data while providing formal client-side privacy guarantees.

Cryptographic mechanisms, such as secure multi-party computation (MPC) and threshold secret sharing, preserve numerical precision without perturbation error~\cite{cunhaneto2023}. However, secure aggregation of high-dimensional neural parameters requires iterative multi-round communication, which burdens resource-constrained IoT edge devices~\cite{cunhaneto2023}.

\subsubsection{State-of-the-Art in Generative PPFL-IDS}
A literature search of IEEE Xplore and SpringerLink (2022--2026) for studies combining federated learning, intrusion detection, and generative models (GAN or VAE) identified five empirical implementations, all from IEEE~\cite{yao2024, he2023, salim2025, jiao2025, shettar2026}. None applies cryptographic secure aggregation to uplink generator updates. Salim et~al.~\cite{salim2025} (global VAE aggregation), Jiao et~al.~\cite{jiao2025} (local CGAN gradients), and Shettar et~al.~\cite{shettar2026} (central aggregator with moments-accountant clipping at $\varepsilon = 4.8$ and $\delta = 10^{-5}$) rely on DP alone. He et~al.~\cite{he2023} combine local gradient noise with blockchain Proof-of-Authority consensus across edge servers, which ensures aggregation integrity and auditability rather than parameter confidentiality.

Yao et~al.~\cite{yao2024} use a hybrid design: proactive dynamic $(t, n)$-threshold secret sharing with $t=4$ and $n=5$ protects only downlink synthetic broadcasts, whereas uplink client gradients rely on Gaussian DP. They report execution measurements for key distribution and sample reconstruction (Table~VI), but secret sharing covers downlink tabular samples, not full model-parameter aggregation. Outside the primary search, Hamouda et~al.~\cite{hamouda2024} likewise omit secure aggregation and identify cryptographic model protection as an open challenge. Because none of these works benchmarks cryptographic aggregation against DP on model weights, their use of DP reflects a design choice rather than a measured trade-off.

The effect of DP perturbation on downstream detection depends on the privacy budget $\varepsilon$, gradient clipping threshold $C$, and minority-class sample density, yet existing studies do not isolate these factors. Utility can remain high at a moderate budget or with large synthetic volumes: Shettar et~al.~\cite{shettar2026} report a 96.8\% detection F1-score under central DP ($\varepsilon = 4.8$), and Hamouda et~al.~\cite{hamouda2024} observe a 0.25-percentage-point accuracy reduction at $\varepsilon = 1.0$ when applying DP-SGD to the downstream global classifier with 50,000 synthetic samples per class. Generative augmentation can also raise rare-class detection from sparse baselines: He et~al.~\cite{he2023} report that CGAN augmentation improved Infiltration F1-score from 33.33\% to as high as 98.01\%, starting from 29 baseline training samples. However, Hamouda et~al.~\cite{hamouda2024} applied DP to the classifier rather than the generator, and neither He et~al.~\cite{he2023} nor Jiao et~al.~\cite{jiao2025} report formal numerical $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ budgets. Because generator-side DP must spread privacy noise across iterative training steps, how strict bounds ($\varepsilon \le 1.0$) interact with extreme class scarcity in federated generative architectures remains an open empirical question.

\subsubsection{Marginal-Based Tabular Synthesis vs. Deep Models}
Marginal-based tabular synthesis offers an alternative to deep generative models. Instead of optimizing non-convex neural network weights by backpropagation, marginal synthesizers select and measure low-order contingency tables and reconstruct the data distribution with probabilistic graphical models. Beyond network IDS research, a cross-domain search of the ACM Digital Library, PMLR, and arXiv identified distributed extensions: Maddock et~al.~\cite{maddock2024} introduce FLAIM, which adapts the Adaptive and Iterative Mechanism (AIM) to distributed clients under zero-concentrated differential privacy ($\rho$-zCDP) and compares DP with secure multi-party computation (DistAIM); Pentyala et~al.~\cite{pentyala2024} present CaPS, a ``DP-in-MPC'' framework in which secret sharing protects marginal selection and measurement; and Li et~al.~\cite{li2026} propose HeteroFedSyn, which compresses two-way marginal dependencies by random projection under distributed perturbation.

Marginal synthesizers avoid the adversarial minimax training instability of GANs but carry their own privacy-utility trade-offs. Iterative selection-and-measurement algorithms (e.g., AIM and Private-PGM) divide the total privacy budget $\varepsilon$ across rounds, and distributing measurements across federated clients compounds perturbation noise~\cite{maddock2024, pentyala2024}. In sparse discrete categories with small contingency cell counts, additive Laplace or Gaussian noise yields a low signal-to-noise ratio. Restricting measurements to low-order marginals (typically two-way dependencies~\cite{li2026}) truncates higher-order multivariate correlations, and continuous flow variables require discretization, which introduces binning artifacts.

Federated marginal generators have so far been evaluated mainly on general-purpose tabular benchmarks from demographic, financial, and biomedical domains (e.g., Adult, Census, Credit Card Fraud, COMPAS, Diabetes)~\cite{maddock2024, pentyala2024, li2026}. FLAIM~\cite{maddock2024} also used the legacy DARPA Intrusion Detection benchmark but evaluated only workload error and negative log-likelihood, not downstream minority-class intrusion classification. Decentralized tabular GANs with cryptographic primitives also exist: Duan et~al.~\cite{duan2022} combine Paillier additively homomorphic encryption for global multimodal distribution encoding with DP during GAN training, but evaluate statistical utility and membership inference resistance on legacy tabular data (KDD Cup 99), not modern protocol telemetry.

\subsubsection{IoT-MQTT Protocol Telemetry and Hypotheses}
IoT-MQTT telemetry combines structured, low-cardinality discrete protocol fields (e.g., message types, QoS levels, retain flags) with continuous flow metrics (e.g., payload lengths, burst durations). Sparse discrete feature combinations form low-count contingency cells, where both generative families risk degradation under DP noise, making this a suitable setting for examining these trade-offs. We hypothesize that, under generator-side DP at strict budgets ($\varepsilon \le 1.0$), a federated marginal synthesizer achieves higher minority-class detection F1-score than a federated deep generative model for attack classes defined by low-order (two-way) discrete header correlations (e.g., MQTT Unauthorized Connect, Malformed Header). We expect the reverse for volumetric or timing-based attacks (e.g., MQTT Publish Floods) governed by multi-attribute continuous flow dynamics. We test this across five random seeds, equalizing privacy budgets by converting all mechanisms to $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP under R{\'e}nyi differential privacy accounting, and compare class-specific F1-scores with a Wilcoxon signed-rank test (treating a margin $|\Delta\mathrm{F1}| \ge 2.0$ percentage points at $p < 0.05$ as statistically significant).

\subsubsection{Identified Research Gaps}
To the best of our knowledge, the systematic survey indicates three distinct research gaps:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Gap 1 -- Marginal Synthesis for Protocol Telemetry:} Deep generative models have been evaluated on MQTT-bearing datasets (e.g., Hamouda et~al.~\cite{hamouda2024}, federated conditional GAN on Edge-IIoTset), but no prior study evaluates a federated marginal-based generator on modern IoT-MQTT telemetry or examines how mixed discrete-continuous features affect marginal synthesis relative to deep models.
    \item \textbf{Gap 2 -- Controlled Architectural Comparison:} No empirical study compares a federated marginal-based synthesizer with a federated deep generative model (e.g., CGAN, VAE) under identical non-IID client partitions, minority-attack imbalance ratios, and calibrated generator-side privacy budgets $(\varepsilon, \delta)$.
    \item \textbf{Gap 3 -- Downstream Detection Trade-offs:} Prior marginal studies compared DP with cryptographic aggregation on workload error, negative log-likelihood, runtime, and communication bandwidth~\cite{maddock2024, pentyala2024}, and decentralized GANs were evaluated for statistical utility and membership inference resistance~\cite{duan2022}. No study quantifies how choosing between DP and cryptographic secure aggregation affects downstream minority-class intrusion detection under matched federated edge conditions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary and Positioning of This Work}
This study directly resolves the aforementioned gaps by presenting PP-FedData. We evaluate communication overhead, synthetic sample fidelity, and downstream minority-class intrusion detection for both marginal and deep generative models under differential privacy and cryptographic secure aggregation on realistic edge IoT networks.
