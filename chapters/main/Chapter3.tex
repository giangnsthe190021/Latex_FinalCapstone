\section{Research Design and Methodology}\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Research Type and Experimental Control}\label{subsec:research_type}
This study is a quantitative, controlled experimental study carried out on one published network-traffic dataset, DoS/DDoS-MQTT-IoT~\cite{alatram2023}. In the terminology of experimental research methodology, it combines controlled experiments with archival research: the independent variables (generative architecture, privacy protection mechanism, and differential privacy budget) are systematically manipulated, while the underlying network traffic comprises recorded real-world traces rather than synthetic traffic generated de novo for this study.

\subsubsection{Research Object}
The object of study is a distributed privacy-preserving data synthesis framework, PP-FedData, which enables multiple IoT/MQTT gateways to collaboratively construct synthetic network telemetry without releasing raw packets. Consequently, downstream Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) can be trained on representative traffic distributions that no single edge gateway could pool independently. PP-FedData is evaluated across four primary dimensions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Downstream Utility:} The detection accuracy of the synthetic data for downstream classifiers;
    \item \textbf{Formal Privacy Guarantees:} The mathematical bounds and empirical leakage resistance of released artefacts;
    \item \textbf{System Overhead:} Communication bandwidth (uplink/downlink) and computational latency per client;
    \item \textbf{Operational Fitness:} The compliance of each configuration with practical edge deployment requirements.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Experimental Control}
Internal validity is maintained by holding all factors invariant except the specific generative route and privacy mechanism under study. Every configuration in the experiment matrix (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_configs}) is evaluated under identical conditions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Identical Data Partitioning:} A locked train/validation/test split partitioned strictly by capture group. The real test split is locked and never accessed during hyperparameter tuning (train: 88,500 rows; validation: 12,000 rows; test: 30,000 rows; class quotas strictly enforced);
    \item \textbf{Consistent Non-IID Skew:} Partitioning of training rows over $K = 5$ edge clients drawn from a Dirichlet distribution with concentration parameter $\alpha = 0.5$~\cite{hsu2019}, fixed deterministically by random seed;
    \item \textbf{Uniform Preprocessing:} A standardized pipeline ($\log(1+x)$ transformation of skewed numeric attributes, standard normal scaling, clipping at $\pm 5\sigma$, and one-hot encoding of categorical headers), producing exactly 60 input feature dimensions;
    \item \textbf{Standardized Downstream Classifiers:} A Random Forest~\cite{breiman2001} with 200 estimators and a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) with hidden layers $[128, 64]$;
    \item \textbf{Multi-Seed Replication:} Identical seed triad $\{0, 1, 2\}$ governing weight initialization, client partitioning, and synthetic sampling;
    \item \textbf{Fixed Real Test Set:} All reported utility metrics are evaluated on the exact same held-out real test set.
\end{itemize}
Under this design, differences in downstream performance are directly attributable to the generative model and privacy mechanism. Extraneous sources of variance, such as hyperparameter optimization and capture group boundaries, are addressed in Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}.

\subsubsection{Evolution of the Design}
The study was originally conceptualized around a federated conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE)~\cite{sohn2015cvae}. Following initial empirical evaluation, the framework transitioned to a marginal-based generator, \textit{FedDP-Marginal}, as its primary generative mechanism, while retaining the federated CVAE as a baseline. This architectural pivot is formally documented in the project deviation log (\texttt{docs/SPEC\_DEVIATIONS.md}, entries O1.9 and O2.3). Maintaining the CVAE configurations in the benchmark matrix ensures a rigorous, head-to-head comparison under identical federation topologies, differential privacy budgets, and secure aggregation environments.

\subsubsection{System Roles}
As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:system_arch}, the PP-FedData architecture comprises three primary entities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Edge Gateways ($K = 5$ FL Clients):} Each gateway observes local MQTT traffic and maintains exclusive custody of its telemetry. Due to non-IID Dirichlet partitioning, individual gateways frequently exhibit severe data sparsity or complete absence of specific rare attack classes.
    \item \textbf{Aggregation Server:} The central coordinator orchestrates communication rounds, computes secure aggregates of client submissions, reconstructs global generative models, and generates synthetic records. The server is modelled as \textit{honest-but-curious} (Section~\ref{subsec:threat_model}).
    \item \textbf{Downstream IDS:} Classifiers (Random Forest and MLP) are trained on generated synthetic traffic (TSTR) or hybrid datasets (TAug, TAugR, local augmentation) and evaluated on the locked real test set.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Synthesis Locality and Server Observability}
Synthetic data records are synthesized centrally at the aggregation server, conditional on specified class labels, either by decoding latent vectors via the global CVAE decoder or by ancestral sampling over the reconstructed global dependency tree and marginal tables. Edge gateways do not generate synthetic traffic locally.

The visibility of client updates to the aggregation server is strictly determined by the active privacy configuration:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SecAgg$^+$ Protocols (M2, M3, MGs, MGr):} Utilizing Flower's SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020, beutel2020}, the server observes only masked parameter vectors, reconstructing strictly the additive sum while learning zero information regarding individual client contributions;
    \item \textbf{Plain FedAvg (B3, M1):} The server observes raw individual client model updates. In M1, updates are perturbed locally via DP-SGD;
    \item \textbf{Local DP (MGl):} Gateways inject full calibrated noise into local tables prior to plaintext transmission, allowing the server to observe individual noisy tables without cryptographic masking;
    \item \textbf{Simulated Distributed DP (MGd, MGb):} The distributed summation is computed within a single execution process to benchmark accounting efficiency.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Comparison of the Two Generator Routes}
Table~\ref{tab:two_routes} contrasts the fundamental architectural mechanisms of the two routes. FedDP-Marginal exchanges discrete frequency counts across three synchronized releases, whereas the federated CVAE exchanges continuous parameter weight vectors over 30 iterative FedAvg rounds.

This operational divergence is decisive for differential privacy accounting. In FedDP-Marginal, each release represents a linear additive summation with bounded global sensitivity; calibrated noise can therefore be divided among clients and reconstituted exactly via secure aggregation. In contrast, DP-SGD~\cite{abadi2016dpsgd} introduces non-linear gradient transformations across successive local optimization epochs, where noise added by one client interacts non-linearly with others; distributed DP is consequently mathematically invalid for the CVAE route (\texttt{SPEC\_DEVIATIONS.md}, entry O2.1).


For FedDP-Marginal, secure summation is implemented via a lightweight client-side patch: stock Flower SecAgg$^+$ transmits masked vectors as 64-bit integers. PP-FedData casts masked vectors to unsigned 32-bit integers (\texttt{uint32}) following modular reduction, leveraging CPU register wrap-around to execute exact modulo $2^{32}$ addition without altering installed external packages (\texttt{SPEC\_DEVIATIONS.md}, entry O2.2).

\subsubsection{Adversary and Trust Assumptions}
The aggregation server is modeled as \textit{honest-but-curious} (semi-honest)~\cite{goldreich2004}: it faithfully executes protocol steps but inspects all incoming payloads to infer private telemetry. Gateways maintain physical control of their own data. We analyze four trust settings:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Base Setting:} All $K$ gateways adhere to protocol and inject their assigned noise share ($1/K$ of the total required variance in distributed variants);
    \item \textbf{$h$ Honest Gateways:} The privacy guarantee is audited under scenarios where only $h \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ clients inject noise, while the remaining $K-h$ clients collude with the server or drop out. Effective $\varepsilon$ increases monotonically as $h$ decreases;
    \item \textbf{Threshold $t$:} Noise is calibrated such that target $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ holds whenever at least $t$ gateways contribute, with each client injecting $1/t$ of total variance (default $t = K$);
    \item \textbf{Local DP (MGl):} Each client adds the full required noise independently~\cite{kasiviswanathan2011}, achieving protection without trusting peer gateways.
\end{enumerate}
Malicious attacks, including data poisoning, model poisoning, and Byzantine corruption, are considered out of scope.


\subsubsection{Roles of SecAgg$^+$ and Differential Privacy}
Table~\ref{tab:protection_mechanisms} emphasizes that Secure Aggregation and Differential Privacy address orthogonal vulnerabilities and cannot be substituted for one another.

\subsubsection{Privacy Unit Granularity}
By default, $\varepsilon$ protects a single network packet record. For FedDP-Marginal, two higher-order privacy units are formalized:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{TCP Stream (\texttt{-strm\{m\}}):} Protects entire bidirectional TCP sessions;
    \item \textbf{Capture Group (\texttt{-cap\{m\}}):} Protects complete contiguous traffic captures.
\end{enumerate}
These guarantees hold strictly under two operational constraints: each unit is hosted entirely on a single client node, and each client clamps contributions to at most $m$ packets per unit. Under these conditions, releasing $T$ contingency tables incurs global sensitivities $\Delta_1 = m \cdot T$ in $\ell_1$ and $\Delta_2 = m\sqrt{T}$ in $\ell_2$ (\texttt{group\_dp.py}). In contrast, under packet-level DP, an attack session spanning $k$ packets is protected only via group privacy bounds, which scale weakly to approximately $k\varepsilon$~\cite{dwork2014}. The training dataset encompasses 76,463 TCP streams and 147 capture groups (ranging between 62 and 1,277 packets per capture).

\subsection{Requirement-Driven Recommendation Framework}\label{subsec:req_rule}

\subsubsection{Motivation and Relation to Heuristic R6}
Standard benchmark studies typically rely on single static selection heuristics. For instance, heuristic rule R6 selects the configuration maximizing Random Forest TAug Macro-F1 subject to membership inference AUC $\le 0.55$, worst-case $\varepsilon \le 5$, and per-round execution time $\le 3\times$ baseline B3. However, static rules cannot adapt to heterogeneous real-world deployments with varying bandwidth, mutual trust, or threat priorities. PP-FedData introduces a requirement-driven decision engine (\texttt{ppfeddata recommend}) operating over an 11-dimensional requirement tuple:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{R} = \langle & \text{Trust}_{\text{srv}}, \text{Trust}_{\text{cli}}, \varepsilon_{\max}, \text{BW}_{\text{rnd}}, \text{BW}_{\text{tot}}, \\
& \text{Rounds}_{\max}, \text{Unit}, \text{Priority}, \text{Recall}_{\text{rare}}^{\min}, \text{MIA}^{\max}, h \rangle
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Five-Stage Recommendation Procedure}
Operating over empirical scorecard metrics extracted directly from the immutable run ledger, the selection algorithm executes five successive filters:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Validity Filter:} Excludes configurations whose operational properties diverge from verified ledger execution;
    \item \textbf{Trust Filter:} If the server is untrusted, only configurations supporting SecAgg$^+$ or Local DP are retained. If client collusion is possible, distributed DP is audited at $h$ honest clients;
    \item \textbf{Constraint Pruning:} Eliminates configurations violating specified ceilings on $\varepsilon$, bandwidth, convergence rounds, or MIA leakage, or failing to satisfy rare-class recall floors;
    \item \textbf{Pareto Optimization:} Extracts non-dominated configurations across utility, privacy, and resource dimensions;
    \item \textbf{Tie-Breaking:} Selects the configuration maximizing the prioritized performance objective (Macro-F1, Binary F1, or Rare-Class Recall).
\end{enumerate}


Table~\ref{tab:scenarios} defines ten representative deployment scenarios used to validate the recommendation framework.

\subsubsection{Dataset Description}
The primary data source of this study is the DoS/DDoS-MQTT-IoT dataset published by Alatram \textit{et al.}~\cite{alatram2023} and obtained from the authors' official Kaggle release~\cite{alatram2023kaggle}. The dataset contains benign IoT traffic together with several denial-of-service (DoS) and distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks that abuse the MQTT protocol~\cite{mqtt311}. Although the release is distributed in several formats, this study uses a structured CSV representation comprising 59,624,826 rows (approximately 13\,GB).

Each row represents a single network packet described by 33 feature columns. The Normal subset carries two additional columns, \texttt{Label} and \texttt{Capture\_ID}; the latter assigns the \texttt{group\_id} used for data splitting, whereas attack traffic derives its \texttt{group\_id} from the name of its source file. An initial inventory confirmed that the row counts match the expected per-class totals. Table~\ref{tab:dataset_composition} summarizes the distribution of normal traffic and the five attack families.


\subsubsection{Data Provenance and Verification}\label{subsubsec:provenance}
Attack traffic is used exactly as published: the 48 attack CSV files exported by the dataset authors~\cite{alatram2023}, whose paths and sizes match the Kaggle release~\cite{alatram2023kaggle}. Because the release provides Normal traffic only as 49 PCAP files, every Normal packet was exported with TShark~4.4.7~\cite{wireshark} into the same 33 columns, using a script maintained in the project repository.

The export is complete, since the number of rows equals the total packet count of all 49 captures (45,580,102 rows), and reproducible, since re-extracting three captures yields byte-identical files. To validate the procedure, it was applied to the attack PCAP files, for which the authors' CSV files serve as ground truth: 23 of the 25 raw features used by the models match the authors' values in at least 99.98\% of rows. The two remaining discrepancies are explained by the authors' export settings, namely a display filter that retains only packets sent to the broker and two-pass dissection; enabling both settings reproduces the authors' rows in all 48 captures. The Normal export therefore contains every packet that the authors' settings would select from the same PCAP files, plus the packets sent by the broker; the implications of this difference are discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}. Table~\ref{tab:provenance} summarizes the verification checks.


\subsubsection{Label Harmonization and Rare Classes}
Target labels are harmonized into a six-class configuration, namely \texttt{NORMAL}, \texttt{BCF}, \texttt{DELAYED}, \texttt{SYN}, \texttt{INVALID}, and \texttt{WILL}, in which the DoS and DDoS variants of the same attack family are merged into a single class. This merging is supported by leakage check C5 (Section~\ref{subsubsec:leakage_checks}), which shows that DoS and DDoS variants within the same family are practically indistinguishable at the packet level. The original 11-class labeling remains available in the data pipeline but is used only for the robustness analysis (Table~\ref{tab:robustness_branches}).

Rare classes are defined as attack classes whose training quota does not exceed 5,000 rows, i.e., \texttt{DELAYED}, \texttt{SYN}, \texttt{INVALID}, and \texttt{WILL}. Their rarity during training is a deliberate consequence of the quota design rather than a lack of raw data: as shown in Table~\ref{tab:dataset_composition}, the raw dataset contains between 270,529 and 601,542 packets for each of these classes. The quotas impose a 60:1 imbalance between the majority class (\texttt{NORMAL}, 60,000 rows) and the smallest minority class (\texttt{WILL}, 1,000 rows), which creates a demanding setting for evaluating synthetic data generators under federated constraints~\cite{he2009}. In contrast, the validation and test sets are class-balanced (2,000 and 5,000 rows per class, respectively), so that the evaluation metrics are not dominated by the majority class.

\subsubsection{Schema Harmonization and Feature Selection}
Preprocessing begins by harmonizing the heterogeneous raw packet exports. Column names are converted to snake case. Wireshark text labels (e.g., \texttt{message\_type}, \texttt{qos\_level\_*}, and \texttt{requested\_qos}) are normalized to canonical codes, and boolean indicators (True/False, Set/Not set) are mapped to 0/1. Redundant columns with the suffix \texttt{\_1} are removed. For multi-valued cells that contain several MQTT messages, only the first element is retained, consistent with the Normal export produced with the TShark \texttt{occurrence=f} option; the \texttt{n\_mqtt\_msgs} column is therefore discarded. Rows are then filtered to those whose protocol is TCP or MQTT, followed by a format audit of time-based tokens.

To prevent data leakage~\cite{kaufman2012, arp2022}, identifier and metadata columns are removed: \texttt{no} (row sequence), \texttt{epoch\_time} (absolute timestamp), and \texttt{time\_since\_reference\_\allowbreak or\_first\_frame} (which reveals the capture start time). The \texttt{stream\_index} column is retained only as grouping metadata and is not used as an input feature. The \texttt{source} column is removed because sender IP addresses would allow a model to memorize host identities instead of learning traffic patterns. Free-text payloads in the \texttt{info} column and the Normal-only \texttt{label} and \texttt{capture\_id} columns are also dropped; the raw dataset contains no MAC address or port columns. After feature selection, the final schema comprises $D = 60$ input features, as detailed in Table~\ref{tab:feature_schema}.


\subsubsection{Group-Based Splitting and Quota Sampling}
The 11 fine-grained subclasses (\texttt{NORMAL} plus the DoS and DDoS variants of the five attack families) are partitioned with a group-based strategy: entire capture groups are assigned to the training, validation, or test split according to a fixed \texttt{split\_seed}. This prevents highly correlated packets from the same TCP stream or capture session from appearing in more than one split~\cite{arp2022}.

However, 8 of the 11 subclasses originate from a single capture file, which precludes a purely group-based assignment. For these subclasses, the validation and test sets are extracted as contiguous blocks of the same capture used for training (\textit{block split}), separated by 1,000-row buffers to mitigate adjacency leakage. TCP streams that cross a block boundary are discarded entirely to preserve stream integrity. This removal eliminates between 1.2\% and 9.9\% of the eligible rows within each affected subclass and introduces a slight sampling bias toward shorter connections.

After splitting, rows are drawn by random sampling without replacement according to the class quotas in Table~\ref{tab:quota}, which emulate the data scarcity faced by individual gateways. The training quotas enforce severe imbalance, whereas the validation and test quotas are balanced. The 30,000-row test set is locked and used only for final reporting; all hyperparameter tuning, design choices, and threshold calibration rely exclusively on the validation set.

\subsubsection{Preprocessing and Encoding}
The preprocessing pipeline is fitted on the training split only; the validation and test sets are transformed with the stored parameters to prevent leakage. Missing or non-applicable numeric values are imputed with 0, and a binary \texttt{\_is\_na} indicator is added for every feature whose missing rate in the training set lies strictly between 0.005 and 0.995.

Numeric features with a skewness greater than 2 are transformed with $\log(1+x)$; time-delta features are first multiplied by 1,000 to convert seconds into milliseconds. Numeric columns are then standardized to zero mean and unit variance and clipped at $\pm 5\sigma$ to limit the influence of extreme outliers. Categorical features are one-hot encoded, retaining the ten most frequent training tokens per column; the remaining valid values are mapped to an \texttt{OTHER} token and missing values to a \texttt{NONE} token.

Normalization statistics, categorical vocabularies, and imputation parameters are fitted centrally on the pooled training set. This constitutes a methodological limitation: it assumes a centralized view of the data prior to federation, and these statistics lie outside the differential privacy budget $\varepsilon$ (Section~\ref{subsec:threat_model}).

\subsection{Federated Partitioning}\label{subsec:partitioning}

\subsubsection{Dirichlet Label-Skew Partitioning}
To model non-identically distributed (non-IID) traffic across edge gateways while preserving protocol coherence, the centralized training set is partitioned across a federation of gateways $\mathcal{K} = \{1, 2, \dots, K\}$, with $K = 5$ in the canonical benchmark. Partitioning follows the label-distribution skew model of Hsu \textit{et al.}~\cite{hsu2019}, parameterized by a symmetric Dirichlet distribution with concentration parameter $\alpha_{\text{dir}}$. For each class $c \in \{1, \dots, C\}$, with $C = 6$ in the canonical label configuration, the assignment proportions across the $K$ gateways are drawn independently as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_c = (p_{c,1}, \dots, p_{c,K}) \sim \mathrm{Dir}(\alpha_{\text{dir}} \mathbf{1}_K),
\label{eq:dirichlet}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{1}_K = (1, \dots, 1) \in \mathbb{R}^K$. The concentration parameter controls statistical heterogeneity~\cite{hsu2019}: small values ($\alpha_{\text{dir}} \to 0$) concentrate each class on few gateways, whereas large values ($\alpha_{\text{dir}} \to \infty$) converge toward an IID allocation. The canonical benchmark adopts $\alpha_{\text{dir}} = 0.5$, under which some gateways naturally receive very few or no instances of the rare attack classes.

\subsubsection{Record-Level versus Unit-Level Partitioning}
Two partitioning regimes are distinguished:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Record-Level Partitioning (Default):} Each packet record $(x, y)$ of class $c$ is assigned to gateway $k$ with probability $p_{c,k}$. Packets from the same TCP stream or capture group may therefore span several gateways, which models an open edge network in which packets of distributed flows traverse different regional edge routers.
    \item \textbf{Unit-Level Partitioning (Group DP):} Reserved for session-level and capture-level differential privacy evaluations (Section~\ref{subsubsec:group_dp}). TCP streams or capture groups are treated as indivisible units: Dirichlet proportions are applied according to the majority class of each unit, and all records of a unit are assigned to a single gateway. This guarantees that no protected entity is fragmented across gateways, giving an unambiguous boundary for group-level contribution bounding.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition Constraints and Safeguards}
The partitioning protocol enforces the following constraints to avoid degenerate simulation states:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Resampling Safeguard:} If any gateway receives fewer than 500 records in total ($\sum_{c} n_{k,c} < 500$), the sampled proportions are rejected and redrawn from $\mathrm{Dir}(\alpha_{\text{dir}} \mathbf{1}_K)$ until all gateways satisfy the floor;
    \item \textbf{Unit-Level Floor:} Under unit-level partitioning, a minimum of 200 records per gateway is enforced so that every node holds sufficient local samples for valid aggregation;
    \item \textbf{Seed-Specific Realization:} Each seed $s \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ instantiates an independent partition draw $\mathbf{p}_c^{(s)}$, and all architectures evaluated on seed $s$ share the same partition instance, which removes partitioning as a confounder;
    \item \textbf{Pre-Partition Split Isolation:} The global train/validation/test split is executed before federated allocation, so the validation and test sets never enter the decentralized training pool. Full session independence across splits remains limited by the block split of single-capture subclasses (Section~\ref{subsec:dataset} and Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Partition Invariance of Linear Marginal Aggregation}
For any contingency query aggregated under SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020}, the global result is an exact unweighted sum of local integer count vectors:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{global}} &= \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbf{h}_k = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_k} \mathbf{e}(x_i) = \sum_{i \in \bigcup_{k} \mathcal{D}_k} \mathbf{e}(x_i),
\end{split}
\label{eq:hist_sum}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}(x_i)$ is the standard basis indicator vector of the bin containing record $x_i$. Because integer addition is commutative and associative, the unperturbed aggregated table is algebraically identical to the centralized table, irrespective of label skew $P_k(Y)$ or covariate shift $P_k(X \mid Y)$.

Evaluating FedDP-Marginal across $\alpha_{\text{dir}} \in \{0.1, 0.5, 10\}$ therefore serves as a \textit{null prediction} (sanity check): without client dropout or entity capping, its aggregated marginals and synthesis fidelity are theoretically invariant to partition skew. In operational networks, heterogeneity affects FedDP-Marginal only through two edge conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Client dropout:} The counts of dropped gateways are omitted from the sum, so local class samples that are absent from surviving gateways under small $\alpha_{\text{dir}}$ are lost;
    \item \textbf{Record-level splits under caps:} With a group contribution cap $m$, an entity spanning several gateways is clipped independently up to $m$ times per gateway, whereas on a single gateway it would be clipped only once.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Overview}
FedDP-Marginal adapts private marginal-based tabular synthesis, specifically the MST framework of McKenna \textit{et al.}~\cite{mckenna2021mst} and the graphical-model principles of PrivBayes~\cite{zhang2017privbayes}, to a federated architecture. It is not a new generative foundation; rather, it adapts marginal-based dependency modeling to the federated setting through secure integer aggregation. Unlike deep federated models that require dozens of rounds over model weights, FedDP-Marginal uses an exact three-release query protocol coordinated via SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020} in Flower~\cite{beutel2020}, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:three_release}.

\subsubsection{Attribute Schema}
The marginal synthesizer and the neural baseline use different feature representations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tabular schema ($d = 37$ attributes):} FedDP-Marginal queries contingency tables over 13 numeric attributes, discretized into $B_{\text{fine}} = 64$ uniform bins over the normalized interval $[-5, 5]$ (consistent with the $\pm 5\sigma$ clipping); 19 binary attributes, comprising 7 protocol flags (\texttt{syn}, \texttt{reset}, \texttt{acknowledgment}, \texttt{retain}, \texttt{clean\_session\_flag\_2}, \texttt{will\_retain}, \texttt{will\_flag}) and 12 \texttt{\_is\_na} indicators; and 5 categorical attributes (\texttt{message\_type}, \texttt{qos\_level\_1}, \texttt{qos\_level\_2}, \texttt{requested\_qos}, \texttt{protocol}) represented directly as discrete variables;
    \item \textbf{One-hot representation ($D = 60$):} The CVAE baseline operates on $\mathbb{R}^{D}$, obtained by expanding the 5 categorical attributes into 28 one-hot indicators ($13 + 19 + 28 = 60$; Table~\ref{tab:feature_schema}).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Three-Release Protocol}
The protocol issues three sequential releases that allocate the privacy budget across complementary granularities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Release 1 (fine univariate marginals per class):} Each gateway tabulates 1-way counts for every attribute $X_i$, $i \in \{1, \dots, d\}$, conditioned on the class label $c$ over $B_{\text{fine}} = 64$ bins, adds its discrete noise share, and submits the result through SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020}. From the aggregated noisy histograms, the server sets data-dependent coarse cutpoints for numeric attributes at empirical quantiles, with $B_{\text{coarse}} \in \{4, 8, 16, 32\}$ (default 16); coarse cutpoints are aligned with fine bin edges.
    \item \textbf{Release 2 (coarse pairwise marginals pooled across classes):} Gateways compute 2-way tables for all $\binom{d}{2} = 666$ attribute pairs over coarse bins, pooled across all classes. Pooling maximizes the count per cell and bounds table size to $B_{\text{coarse}}^2$, which limits the relative impact of privacy noise on dependency estimation.
    \item \textbf{Release 3 (tree-edge marginals conditioned on class):} Using the structure derived from Release~2, the server requests joint tables for the $d - 1$ edges of the global Chow--Liu tree conditioned on $y = c$ over coarse bins (cardinality $B_{\text{coarse}}^2 \cdot C$, which avoids noise swamping on rare classes), together with the class-conditional root table over fine bins.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Single Global Chow--Liu Tree}
FedDP-Marginal constructs a \textit{single} global Chow--Liu dependency tree~\cite{chow1968} pooled across classes rather than one tree per class. From the post-processed Release~2 tables, the server computes the empirical mutual information (MI) between attributes and extracts the maximum spanning tree with Kruskal's algorithm~\cite{kruskal1956}:
\begin{equation}
T^\star = \arg\max_{T} \sum_{(i,j) \in T} \hat{I}(X_i; X_j),
\label{eq:chow_liu}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\hat{I}(X_i; X_j) = \sum_{a, b} \hat{p}(a, b) \log \frac{\hat{p}(a, b)}{\hat{p}(a)\, \hat{p}(b)}.
\label{eq:mi}
\end{equation}
Following the post-processing approach of McKenna \textit{et al.}~\cite{mckenna2021mst}, empirical probabilities are obtained from the noisy pairwise counts $c_{i,j}$ by non-negative thresholding, additive (Laplace) smoothing $\alpha_{\text{sm}}$, and normalization:
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}(a, b) = \frac{\max(0, c_{i,j}(a, b)) + \alpha_{\text{sm}}}{\sum_{a', b'} \left[\max(0, c_{i,j}(a', b')) + \alpha_{\text{sm}}\right]},
\label{eq:phat}
\end{equation}
and the univariate terms $\hat{p}(a)$ and $\hat{p}(b)$ are obtained by summing out the corresponding dimension. By the post-processing property of differential privacy~\cite{dwork2014}, deriving $T^\star$ consumes no additional privacy budget.

\textit{Pooling trade-off.} Class-conditional MI $\hat{I}(X_i; X_j \mid Y = c)$ would align more closely with class-conditional synthesis. However, splitting Release~2 by class leaves the per-cell sensitivity unchanged while dividing the per-cell signal by roughly $C$, and the total noise mass across the $C\binom{d}{2}$ tables grows $C$-fold; the per-cell signal-to-noise ratio therefore degrades by a factor of $C$ and corrupts edge selection. FedDP-Marginal deliberately pools pairwise counts to maximize the signal-to-noise ratio of the skeleton $T^\star$. The pooled MI thus reflects the overall dependency structure, which is dominated by \texttt{NORMAL} traffic (67.8\% of the training quota), while class-specific relationships are captured by the class-conditional edge tables of Release~3.

\textit{Cardinality bias.} Plug-in MI on noisy tables has a finite-sample bias proportional to the table degrees of freedom $(|X_i| - 1)(|X_j| - 1)$: up to $(16 - 1)^2 = 225$ for pairs of numeric attributes with $B_{\text{coarse}} = 16$, compared with $1$ for binary pairs. Kruskal's algorithm on unnormalized MI therefore tends to favor edges between high-cardinality numeric attributes, and additive smoothing, which adds pseudo-mass $\alpha_{\text{sm}}|X_i||X_j|$, dilutes probabilities more strongly in larger tables. The Miller--Madow correction~\cite{miller1955}
\begin{equation}
\hat{I}_{\text{MM}}(X_i; X_j) = \hat{I}(X_i; X_j) - \frac{(|X_i| - 1)(|X_j| - 1)}{2\hat{N}},
\label{eq:miller_madow}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{N} = \sum_{a,b} \max(0, c_{i,j}(a, b))$, adjusts for asymptotic multinomial sampling bias but does not account for additive Skellam noise or the non-linear truncation $\max(0, \cdot)$. The canonical pipeline retains uncorrected plug-in MI with $B_{\text{coarse}} \leq 16$ as the baseline heuristic. To quantify the effect of cardinality bias on structure discovery separately from downstream utility, an ablation comparing plug-in MI, Miller--Madow MI, and normalized MI ($\hat{I}(X_i; X_j)/\sqrt{H(X_i) H(X_j)}$), together with a tree-edge stability check across partition seeds, is included in search space~(d) (Section~\ref{subsec:hpo}).

\subsubsection{Class-Conditional Sampling}
Synthetic records of class $c$ are generated by traversing $T^\star$ in topological root-to-leaf order~\cite{chow1968}:
\begin{equation}
\hat{P}(\mathbf{x} \mid y = c) = \hat{P}(x_r \mid y = c) \prod_{(u, v) \in T^\star} \hat{P}(x_v \mid x_u, y = c),
\label{eq:tree_factorization}
\end{equation}
where $r$ denotes the root attribute. Sampling proceeds as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Root selection and sampling:} For MGs, the root is the attribute maximizing $\sum_{j \neq r} \hat{I}(X_r; X_j)$; it is sampled from its class-conditional fine distribution of Release~1, $x_r \sim \hat{P}(X_r \mid y = c)$;
    \item \textbf{Conditional propagation:} Each child is sampled given its parent, $x_v \sim \hat{P}(X_v \mid X_u = x_u, y = c)$, from noisy counts that are thresholded at zero and smoothed with $\alpha_{\text{sm}}$;
    \item \textbf{Continuous refinement:} For numeric attributes, sampled coarse bins are refined to fine bins in proportion to the fine class histogram of Release~1 (whose bins nest within the coarse bins), followed by uniform interpolation within $[b_{\text{low}}, b_{\text{high}}]$;
    \item \textbf{Pipeline inversion:} The resulting vectors pass through the same inverse transformation (inverse standardization and inverse $\log(1+x)$) as the CVAE baseline.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Sensitivity, Budget Allocation, and Consistency Refinement}
\textit{Neighboring relation and sensitivity.} Accounting uses unbounded adjacency, i.e., datasets $D \sim D'$ differing by the addition or removal of one packet record. A single record increments exactly one cell in each of the $d$ tables of Release~1, the $\binom{d}{2}$ tables of Release~2, and the $d$ tables of Release~3 ($d - 1$ edge tables plus the root table). For a release of $T$ tables, the global sensitivities are therefore $\Delta_1 = T$ and $\Delta_2 = \sqrt{T}$. Under bounded DP (record replacement within a class), they become $\Delta_1^{\text{repl}} = 2T$ and $\Delta_2^{\text{repl}} = \sqrt{2T}$.

\textit{Budget split.} The budget is distributed across releases by a split vector $\mathbf{s} = (s_1, s_2, s_3)$ with $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 1$, defaulting to $(0.3, 0.1, 0.6)$. For the Gaussian variant (MGd), the zCDP parameter is split as $\rho_r = s_r \rho$, giving per-release noise standard deviation $\sigma_r = \Delta_{2,r}/\sqrt{2 s_r \rho}$. For the Skellam variant (MGs), the noise parameter of release $r$ is $\mu_{\text{sk},r} = \Delta_{2,r}^2/(2 s_r \kappa)$, where the global factor $\kappa > 0$ is found by binary search. Under the default split, Release~2 has $\Delta_{2,2}^2 = \binom{37}{2} = 666$ with $s_2 = 0.1$ and Release~1 has $\Delta_{2,1}^2 = 37$ with $s_1 = 0.3$, so $\mu_{\text{sk},2}/\mu_{\text{sk},1} = (666/0.1)/(37/0.3) = 54$. Release~2 thus carries about $54\times$ the per-cell noise variance of Release~1, which makes coarse binning ($B_{\text{coarse}} \leq 16$) necessary to preserve the signal-to-noise ratio.

\textit{Consistency refinement (MGr).} To enforce consistency without consuming privacy budget, MGr applies the following post-processing steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inverse-variance count merging:} Merging is performed in count space per class before normalization. Fine Release~1 counts are collapsed to coarse bins; a coarse bin $b$ of $X_u$ containing $k_b$ fine bins has variance $k_b \sigma_1^2$. Projecting an incident Release~3 edge table $(X_u, X_v)$ onto $X_u$ by summing over the $|X_v|$ coarse bins of $X_v$ yields an independent estimate with variance $|X_v| \sigma_3^2$. A non-root attribute with degree $d(u)$ in $T^\star$ thus has $1 + d(u)$ independent estimators; the root additionally has the class-conditional fine root table of Release~3 (variance $k_b \sigma_3^2$) and merges $2 + d(r)$ estimators. Following McKenna \textit{et al.}~\cite{mckenna2021mst}, estimators are merged bin-wise as
    \begin{equation}
    \hat{C}^{\text{mrg}}_{u}(b \mid c) = \frac{\sum_{j} w_{u,j}(b)\, \hat{C}_{u,j}(b \mid c)}{\sum_{j} w_{u,j}(b)},
    \label{eq:ivw_merge}
    \end{equation}
    with $w_{u,j}(b) = 1/\mathrm{Var}(\hat{C}_{u,j}(b \mid c))$, and then normalized to probabilities $\hat{P}^{\text{mrg}}_u(b) = \hat{C}^{\text{mrg}}_u(b)/\sum_{b'} \hat{C}^{\text{mrg}}_u(b')$;
    \item \textbf{Non-negative truncation:} Merged marginals and noisy Release~3 tables are projected onto the non-negative orthant and normalized;
    \item \textbf{Iterative proportional fitting (IPF):} Each edge table $(X_u, X_v \mid y = c)$ is adjusted with IPF~\cite{deming1940, csiszar1975} to match the merged marginals of $X_u$ and $X_v$, which guarantees margin consistency;
    \item \textbf{Smoothing and root selection:} Additive smoothing $\alpha_{\text{sm}} \in \{0.1, 1, 10\}$ is applied, and the root is selected by the maximum sum of noisy MI.
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Second-order Bayesian network (MGb).} This ablation adapts PrivBayes~\cite{zhang2017privbayes}: parent pairs $(u_1, u_2)$ are selected greedily from Release~2 MI subject to acyclicity. The number of tables touched per record remains $d$, so record sensitivity is unchanged, while individual tables grow to accommodate two parents.

\subsubsection{Secure Transport via SecAgg$^+$}
For MGs and MGr, updates are transmitted through Flower SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020} over the integer ring $\mathbb{Z}_M$ with $M = 2^{32}$ (\texttt{uint32}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item Client updates use \texttt{max\_weight} with unit scaling, and the quantization range is set to twice the clipping range, which guarantees exact integer transmission without floating-point rounding error;
    \item Under record-level DP, \texttt{clipping\_range} $= 100{,}000$, which exceeds the largest possible cell count ($N = 88{,}500$). Under group DP, it is set to $C_{\text{clip}} = N + 6\sqrt{\mu_{\text{sk}}^{\text{group}}/K}$, which bounds both the data mass and the $6\sigma$ tail of the discrete noise, so that the probability of a perturbed count exceeding $C_{\text{clip}}$ is below $10^{-9}$;
    \item Perturbed counts $c_{k,j} = x_{k,j} + \xi_{k,j} \pmod{2^{32}}$ are recovered at the server by centered lifting, $\mathrm{lift}(S) = S$ if $S < 2^{31}$ and $S - 2^{32}$ otherwise. Since $\sum_k x_{k,j} \leq N$ and, under record-level DP, $\sigma_{\text{agg}} = \sqrt{\mu_{\text{sk}}} \leq 400$, overflow is impossible in practice. Under group DP with cap $m = 100$, $\mu_{\text{sk}}^{\text{group}} \leq 1.6 \times 10^{9}$, so $\sigma_{\text{agg}} \leq 4 \times 10^{4}$ (per-client share $\sigma_k = \sqrt{\mu_{\text{sk}}/K} \leq 1.8 \times 10^{4}$), and $|S| \leq N + 6\sigma_{\text{agg}} \approx 3.3 \times 10^{5} \ll 2^{31} \approx 2.15 \times 10^{9}$; the probability of modular wrap-around is below $10^{-50}$;
    \item The Gaussian variant MGd is simulated in-process over $\mathbb{R}$, and the Local DP variant MGl transmits unshared noisy tables without SecAgg$^+$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Privacy Accounting}\label{subsec:privacy_accounting}

\subsubsection{Analytical zCDP Accounting for the Gaussian Comparator (MGd)}
MGd is accounted with analytical zero-concentrated differential privacy (zCDP)~\cite{bun2016}. For release $r \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ with noise standard deviation $\sigma_r$ and $\ell_2$ sensitivity $\Delta_{2,r}$, the accumulated parameter $\rho$ and the resulting $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ guarantee are~\cite{bun2016}
\begin{equation}
\rho = \sum_{r=1}^{3} \frac{\Delta_{2,r}^2}{2\sigma_r^2}, \qquad \varepsilon = \rho + 2\sqrt{\rho \ln(1/\delta)},
\label{eq:zcdp}
\end{equation}
with $\rho$ split across releases by $\mathbf{s} = (0.3, 0.1, 0.6)$ by default. The analytical zCDP conversion is intrinsically looser than numerical RDP conversion~\cite{balle2020}; at identical nominal $\varepsilon$, utility differences between MGd and MGs therefore partly reflect the tightness of the accountant rather than the noise mechanism alone.

\subsubsection{Skellam Mechanism and Accounting (MGs, MGr)}
Nominal guarantees are evaluated at $\varepsilon \in \{1, 5, 10\}$ with $\delta = 10^{-5}$. The Skellam distribution $\mathrm{Sk}(\mu_{\text{sk}}) = \mathrm{Pois}(\mu_{\text{sk}}/2) - \mathrm{Pois}(\mu_{\text{sk}}/2)$ is the difference of two independent Poisson variables, with zero mean and variance $\mu_{\text{sk}}$~\cite{skellam1946, agarwal2021}. Because it is closed under summation, integer noise shares generated by the gateways add up exactly under modular addition over $\mathbb{Z}_{2^{32}}$~\cite{agarwal2021}. Accounting uses the R\'enyi differential privacy (RDP) bound of Agarwal \textit{et al.}~\cite[Corollary~3.6]{agarwal2021}: for integer order $\lambda > 1$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\varepsilon(\lambda) \leq{}& \frac{\lambda \Delta_2^2}{2\mu_{\text{sk}}} \\
&+ \min\!\left( \frac{(2\lambda - 1)\Delta_2^2 + 6\Delta_1}{4\mu_{\text{sk}}^2}, \frac{3\Delta_1}{2\mu_{\text{sk}}} \right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:skellam_rdp}
\end{equation}
RDP is composed additively over the three releases~\cite{mironov2017rdp}, $\varepsilon_{\text{total}}(\lambda) = \sum_{r=1}^{3} \varepsilon_r(\lambda)$, and converted to $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ with the numerical RDP accountant of Opacus~\cite{yousefpour2021}. Because \eqref{eq:skellam_rdp} holds only for integer orders, the accountant grid is restricted to $\lambda \in \{2, 3, \dots, 128\}$. A global calibration factor for $\mu_{\text{sk}}$ is found by binary search so that the composed guarantee matches the target $(\varepsilon, \delta)$.

\subsubsection{Trust Models and Noise Partitioning}
Noise calibration is evaluated under three trust assumptions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Distributed DP ($t = K$, canonical):} Each gateway injects $1/K$ of the total variance, $\mu_{\text{sk},k} = \mu_{\text{sk}}/K$. The nominal guarantee holds when all $K$ gateways are honest; when only $h < K$ remain honest, the effective $\varepsilon(h)$ is recomputed with the Opacus RDP accountant from the reduced aggregate variance $\frac{h}{K}\mu_{\text{sk}}$, $h \in \{1, \dots, K\}$.
    \item \textbf{Threshold DP ($t \leq K$):} Each gateway injects $\mu_{\text{sk},k} = \mu_{\text{sk}}/t$, so the target guarantee holds whenever at least $t$ gateways participate honestly. Under SecAgg$^+$, masks are shared via Shamir secret sharing with reconstruction threshold $T_{\text{recon}}$~\cite{bell2020}; to prevent colluding gateways from reconstructing honest gateways' masking seeds, the number of colluders $c = K - t$ must satisfy $c < T_{\text{recon}}$. The evaluated configurations are: $t = 5$ (canonical distributed DP, $c = 0$, $T_{\text{recon}} = 3$); $t = 3$ (canonical threshold DP, $c \leq 2 < T_{\text{recon}} = 3$, tolerating up to two dropouts or two semi-honest colluders while preserving aggregate-only visibility); and $t = 2$ (hardened threshold DP), which tolerates $c = 3$ colluders only if $T_{\text{recon}}$ is raised to $4$ (leaving one dropout of tolerance), since with $T_{\text{recon}} = 3$ three colluders could reconstruct honest masks and reduce the guarantee to Local DP.
    \item \textbf{Local DP (MGl):} Each gateway injects the full variance, $\mu_{\text{sk},k} = \mu_{\text{sk}}$. No trust in peers or the server is required and SecAgg$^+$ is not used, but the aggregate carries $K\mu_{\text{sk}}$ noise variance.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Group Privacy and Contribution Bounding}\label{subsubsec:group_dp}
To protect against session tracing or capture re-identification, group differential privacy is enforced by scaling sensitivities with the per-unit record cap $m$~\cite{dwork2014}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_1^{\text{group}} = m \Delta_1, \qquad \Delta_2^{\text{group}} = m \Delta_2.
\label{eq:group_sens}
\end{equation}
The Gaussian standard deviation then scales as $\sigma^{\text{group}} = m\sigma$ and the Skellam parameter as $\mu_{\text{sk}}^{\text{group}} \approx m^2 \mu_{\text{sk}}$. Group DP holds formally when (i) each protected unit (TCP stream or capture group) resides entirely on one gateway through unit-level partitioning, and (ii) each gateway caps every unit to at most $m$ records. Under default record-level DP ($m = 1$), a unit of $\ell$ records incurs an inflated group bound. Because the approximate-DP group relaxation $\ell e^{(\ell-1)\varepsilon}\delta$ becomes vacuous for $\ell \geq 3$ at $\varepsilon = 10$, the operative group guarantee is computed via zCDP scaling for Gaussian mechanisms~\cite{bun2016},
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\text{group}} = \ell^2 \rho,
\label{eq:group_zcdp}
\end{equation}
converted to $(\varepsilon_{\text{group}}, \delta)$ via \eqref{eq:zcdp}, or by re-evaluating \eqref{eq:skellam_rdp} with $\Delta \rightarrow \ell\Delta$ for Skellam mechanisms. The caps $m \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ for streams and $m \in \{25, 50, 100\}$ for capture groups represent target privacy policies rather than tuned model parameters.

\subsubsection{Validity Constraints and Verification}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Linear aggregation constraint:} Distributed DP under secure addition is valid only when each release is a single additive sum; it cannot be applied to iterative multi-epoch DP-SGD in the CVAE route;
    \item \textbf{Uniform protection rule:} Noise is never reduced selectively for rare classes, as this would weaken the guarantee for minority-class records below the announced $\varepsilon$;
    \item \textbf{Plaintext leakage disclosure:} Flower transmits client dataset sizes $n_i$ (\texttt{num\_examples}) in plaintext, outside the DP perimeter;
    \item \textbf{Independent verification:} All reported $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ values are recomputed independently (\texttt{eval/dp\_check.py}); an audit flag (F5) is raised if $|\varepsilon_{\text{recomp}} - \varepsilon|/\varepsilon > 0.10$.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{DP-SGD Accounting for the Federated CVAE}
The deep baseline is trained with DP-SGD~\cite{abadi2016dpsgd} adapted to federated learning: at each local step, every gateway clips per-sample gradients to norm $C$ and perturbs the minibatch sum with Gaussian noise (Section~\ref{subsec:cvae}), implemented with Opacus~1.6.0~\cite{yousefpour2021}. Minibatches are formed by Poisson sampling with ratio $q_k = B_k/n_k$, where $B_k = \min(B_{\text{batch}}, n_k)$ ensures $q_k \leq 1$. The local privacy loss $\varepsilon_k$ is accumulated over $R$ rounds and $E$ local epochs with the RDP accountant for the sampled Gaussian mechanism~\cite{mironov2019}.

The noise multiplier $\sigma_k$ is calibrated per gateway from its local dataset size $n_k$ and step count. Under skewed partitions, gateways with very small allocations ($n_k \approx 500 \leq B_{\text{batch}}$) have $q_k = 1$ and lose privacy amplification by subsampling, so their $\sigma_k$ must be considerably larger. Because of discrete step counts and finite calibration grids, the realized $\varepsilon_k$ vary slightly across gateways; federation-wide privacy is therefore reported conservatively as the worst case~\cite{mironov2019},
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\text{effective}} = \max_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \varepsilon_k,
\label{eq:eps_effective}
\end{equation}
together with the federation median, with $\delta < 1/n_k$ for all gateways. In configuration M3, SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020} protects model updates in transit, but noise is still injected locally without cross-client noise sharing. The CVAE route therefore operates under local DP-SGD, whereas FedDP-Marginal benefits from distributed DP under secure aggregation.

\subsubsection{Formal Privacy Perimeter}
The formal DP perimeter is defined as follows (cf.\ Section~\ref{subsec:threat_model}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Protected by $\varepsilon$:} All 37 attributes (and their 64-bin discrete states) of client training records. Coarse cutpoints are derived from the noisy Release~1 histograms and are therefore covered by post-processing~\cite{dwork2014};
    \item \textbf{Public side information outside $\varepsilon$:} Class labels $y$ and global class quotas; client sample counts $n_i$; and global preprocessing parameters (means, standard deviations, $\log(1+x)$ choices, and $\pm 5\sigma$ clipping bounds) computed centrally on the training data. Treating normalization parameters as public is a common convention in tabular benchmarks but lies technically outside the formal accounting (Section~\ref{subsec:limitations});
    \item \textbf{Selection leakage:} Hyperparameters are selected on the unperturbed validation set without private selection (Section~\ref{subsec:hpo}); reported $\varepsilon$ values are nominal training guarantees, not end-to-end guarantees that include hyperparameter selection;
    \item \textbf{Relaxation parameter:} $\delta = 10^{-5}$ is fixed for all evaluations ($1/n \approx 1.13 \times 10^{-5}$ for $n = 88{,}500$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Federated CVAE Baseline}\label{subsec:cvae}

\subsubsection{Architecture and Loss}
The deep generative baseline is a label-conditional CVAE~\cite{sohn2015cvae}. The encoder $q_\phi(z \mid x, y)$ maps features $x \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$ ($D = 60$) and one-hot labels $y \in \{0, 1\}^{C}$ to latent parameters $(\mu_z, \log \sigma_z^2) \in \mathbb{R}^{2d_z}$, and the decoder $p_\theta(x \mid z, y)$ reconstructs the input. The model omits batch normalization, which is required for per-sample gradient independence so that Opacus can clip per-example gradients without inter-sample coupling~\cite{yousefpour2021}; its parameters form a fixed-size vector compatible with FedAvg and SecAgg$^+$. The model minimizes the $\beta$-weighted conditional variational loss~\cite{sohn2015cvae, higgins2017}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{CVAE}} ={}& \sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}_{\text{num}}} \|x_b - \hat{x}_b\|_2^2 + \sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}_{\text{bin}}} \mathrm{BCE}(x_b, \hat{x}_b) \\
&+ \sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{cat}}} \mathrm{CE}(x_g, \hat{x}_g) \\
&+ \beta\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q_\phi(z \mid x, y) \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})\big),
\end{split}
\label{eq:cvae_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}_{\text{num}}$, $\mathcal{B}_{\text{bin}}$, and $\mathcal{G}_{\text{cat}}$ denote the numeric, binary, and categorical feature blocks. The weight $\beta$ is warmed up linearly from 0 to its target of 0.5 over the first 10 local epochs (the first 5 global rounds with $E = 2$), and training is monitored by early stopping on validation loss. The canonical parameters are $d_z = 16$, hidden layers $[128, 64]$, learning rate $\eta = 10^{-3}$, batch size $B_{\text{batch}} = 256$, and $\beta = 0.5$.

\subsubsection{Federated Optimization}
Training uses FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017} over $R = 30$ global rounds with $E = 2$ local epochs per round:
\begin{equation}
w^{r+1} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{n_k}{\sum_{j} n_j} w_k^{r+1}.
\label{eq:fedavg}
\end{equation}
Adam optimizer states are reinitialized at the start of each round, client updates are aggregated in deterministic client-ID order for exact reproducibility, and the federation is simulated with Flower~1.39.0~\cite{beutel2020} using Ray~2.59.0 actors that share CPU resources on a single machine.

\subsubsection{DP-SGD Execution}
When DP is enforced (M1 and M3), gateways run DP-SGD with Opacus~1.6.0~\cite{yousefpour2021}. At each local step, per-sample gradients are clipped to norm $C$ and the minibatch sum is perturbed with Gaussian noise~\cite{abadi2016dpsgd}:
\begin{equation}
\bar{g}_i = \frac{g_i}{\max(1, \|g_i\|_2 / C)},
\label{eq:dpsgd_clip}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\tilde{g} = \frac{1}{B_k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{B_k} \bar{g}_i + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_k^2 C^2 \mathbf{I})\right),
\label{eq:dpsgd_noise}
\end{equation}
where $B_k = \min(B_{\text{batch}}, n_k)$ provides a valid normalization constant for small gateways.

\subsubsection{SecAgg$^+$ Configuration and Overflow Bounds}
For M2 and M3, updates are aggregated with Flower's \texttt{SecAggPlusWorkflow} and \texttt{secaggplus\_mod}~\cite{bell2020}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Topology:} \texttt{num\_shares} $= 5$ and \texttt{reconstruction\_threshold} $= 3$, tolerating up to two dropped gateways per round;
    \item \textbf{Bounds:} \texttt{clipping\_range} $= 16$ (validated against the maximum absolute parameter magnitude), uniform quantization over $Q = 2^{22}$ levels, \texttt{max\_weight} $= 100{,}000$ (bounding $n_k \leq N = 88{,}500$), and modulus $M = 2^{32}$;
    \item \textbf{Weight pre-normalization:} To realize exact weighted FedAvg \eqref{eq:fedavg} without modular overflow, each gateway scales its update by its relative weight $p_k = n_k/\sum_{j \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{active}}} n_j \in (0, 1]$, using the plaintext sample sizes of the active set $\mathcal{K}_{\text{active}}$. The clipped update $\bar{\mathbf{w}}_k$ ($\|\bar{\mathbf{w}}_k\|_\infty \leq 16$) becomes $\mathbf{u}_k = p_k \bar{\mathbf{w}}_k$, which still satisfies $\|\mathbf{u}_k\|_\infty \leq 16$, and is quantized to $\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_k \in \{0, \dots, Q - 1\}$. The server receives
    \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{S} = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{active}}} \tilde{\mathbf{u}}_k \pmod{2^{32}},
    \label{eq:secagg_sum}
    \end{equation}
    and, since each entry is at most $Q - 1$,
    \begin{equation}
    \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{active}}} \tilde{u}_k \leq K(Q - 1) \approx 2.097 \times 10^{7} \ll 2^{31}.
    \label{eq:secagg_bound}
    \end{equation}
    The server applies centered lifting and affine dequantization to recover $\sum_k p_k \bar{\mathbf{w}}_k$ directly; because $\sum_k p_k = 1$, no division by $\sum_k n_k$ is needed. Scaling by raw counts would instead overflow, since $\sum_k n_k(Q - 1) \approx 88{,}500 \times 2^{22} \approx 3.71 \times 10^{11} > 2^{32}$;
    \item \textbf{Validation:} Tests T-SA1 (deviation from plain FedAvg $\leq 10^{-4}$), T-SA2 (masked vectors uncorrelated with true weights and uniformly distributed), and T-SA3 (robustness under simulated dropouts). SecAgg$^+$ provides transport privacy only and receives no credit in DP accounting.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Synthetic Sample Generation}
After training, the encoder is discarded and samples are generated by drawing $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ and decoding with $p_\theta(x \mid z, y = c)$~\cite{sohn2015cvae}. Outputs are sampled per feature block: Bernoulli sampling for binary features, softmax sampling for categorical blocks, and inverse standardization for numeric features. Two decoders are considered. The \textit{plain decoder} uses the final decoder activations directly, stays within the formal DP guarantee, and is the canonical format for all DP benchmarks. The \textit{residual-noise decoder} restores class-conditional feature variances estimated on centralized training data (outside $\varepsilon$) and serves only as a non-private reference. The O1 optimization round additionally evaluates DP-computed residual statistics (budget fraction \texttt{stat\_frac}$\,\times\,\varepsilon$) and client class weighting (\texttt{cw\_power}). \subsubsection{Optimization Protocol and Selection Integrity}
Hyperparameters are optimized with Optuna~\cite{akiba2019} using the Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE)~\cite{bergstra2011}, exclusively on the validation split. The objective is downstream IDS utility: the validation macro-F1 of a Random Forest~\cite{breiman2001} (100 trees during tuning to limit search time; 200 trees in the final protocols) trained on 5,000 synthetic rows per class and evaluated on real validation traffic (TSTR). The test split remains locked and is evaluated only once on the finalized configurations.

Selecting hyperparameters on unperturbed validation data leaks information about the training set through the trial models. Private selection algorithms~\cite{mcsherry2007, liu2019, papernot2022} can bound this cost; this study reports the nominal training $\varepsilon$ and documents the trial budgets explicitly to enable such accounting.

\subsubsection{Decoupled Search Spaces}
Tuning is decoupled into five search spaces (Table~\ref{tab:search_spaces}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Space (a), centralized non-DP CVAE:} 30 TPE trials on centralized data over 30 epochs (output \texttt{configs/best\_cvae.yaml});
    \item \textbf{Space (b), CVAE DP proxy:} To limit cost, initial DP-SGD tuning runs 30 trials on a single proxy client holding 20\% of the data at $\varepsilon = 5$ (one seed per trial). Top candidates are verified in true federated training (\texttt{verify-dp}), selected by validation macro-F1 with ties within 0.01 broken by the lowest validation loss (output \texttt{configs/best\_cvae\_dp.yaml}, family M1d-t21);
    \item \textbf{Space (c), full-scale federated DP CVAE (O1):} Full federated optimization over 5 clients on seed 0, with 30, 28, and 12 trials for $\varepsilon = 5$, $1$, and $10$, respectively; width 256 was pruned due to memory limits (output \texttt{configs/best\_cvae\_dp\_full.yaml}, configurations M1o and M3o);
    \item \textbf{Space (d), FedDP-Marginal:} Discrete grid search scored by the mean of three seeds on the validation set, covering coarse bins, budget splits, the dependency metric (plug-in MI, Miller--Madow MI~\cite{miller1955}, or normalized MI) with a tree-stability check, MGb options (parent search, rare-class bins, fine bin counts), and MGr refinement options. Tree stability is measured by the Jaccard similarity~\cite{jaccard1912} of edge sets across partition seeds,
    \begin{equation}
    J(T_a^\star, T_b^\star) = \frac{|E(T_a^\star) \cap E(T_b^\star)|}{|E(T_a^\star) \cup E(T_b^\star)|}.
    \label{eq:jaccard}
    \end{equation}
    Policy regimes for group caps ($m_{\text{stream}} \in \{1, 2, 4\}$, $m_{\text{capture}} \in \{25, 50, 100\}$) and honest thresholds ($t \in \{5, 3\}$, or $t = 2$ with $T_{\text{recon}} = 4$) are fixed in \texttt{best\_group\_dp.yaml};
    \item \textbf{Space (e), downstream IDS:} Selection between Random Forest (200 trees) and MLP (hidden layers $[128, 64]$, early stopping) per protocol by validation macro-F1; the TAugR ratio (seed 0); and class-probability weights $w_c$ via a two-round coordinate search averaged over three seeds.
\end{enumerate}


\subsubsection{Multi-Seed Screening Rule}
For architectural ablations within space~(d), candidates are screened with an adaptation of the one-standard-error rule~\cite{hastie2009}:
\begin{equation}
\overline{\text{F1}}_{\text{cand}} > \overline{\text{F1}}_{\text{base}} + \mathrm{sd}_{\text{seeds}}(\text{base}),
\label{eq:screening}
\end{equation}
where $\overline{\text{F1}}$ denotes the mean validation macro-F1 over seeds $\{0, 1, 2\}$, the sample standard deviation uses $\text{ddof} = 1$, and the baseline is the canonical MGs generator at the same $\varepsilon$. This condition is a coarse preliminary filter for pruning unpromising configurations, not a formal hypothesis test; formal comparisons rely on the paired bootstrap decision rule~\cite{efron1993} in \eqref{eq:decision_rule}.

Because the screening rule compares unpaired three-seed means, under the null hypothesis of equal mean and variance $\sigma^2$ the difference of means has variance $2\sigma^2/3$. The offset $\mathrm{sd}_{\text{seeds}}(\text{base}) \approx \sigma$ thus corresponds to $z = \sigma/\sqrt{2/3}\,\sigma = \sqrt{1.5} \approx 1.22$, i.e., a one-sided false-pass probability of $1 - \Phi(1.22) \approx 0.11$. In a grid of at least 12 candidates, about 1.3 null candidates are expected to pass by chance, which confirms that \eqref{eq:screening} acts only as a high-throughput sieve.

The tuning protocols are asymmetric: owing to the computational cost of DP-SGD, CVAE searches used one seed per trial (space~(c) tuned on seed 0 and then evaluated on all seeds), whereas FedDP-Marginal selection used the mean of three seeds on validation data. Final test evaluations are executed on the locked test split over seeds $\{0, 1, 2\}$, and all validation and test scores are reported in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Protocols}
The evaluation determines how well an IDS trained under different data regimes performs on authentic network traffic. Regardless of the training data, every protocol is evaluated on the same locked 30,000-row real test set (Section~\ref{subsec:dataset}), which provides a consistent benchmark across configurations. Building on the train-on-synthetic, test-on-real paradigm~\cite{esteban2017}, the following protocols are defined:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TRTR (Train Real, Test Real):} Upper-bound reference in which the IDS is trained on the pooled real training data, including variants with balanced class weights (B1a) and SMOTE oversampling~\cite{chawla2002} (B1b);
    \item \textbf{TSTR (Train Synthetic, Test Real):} Measures the standalone value of synthetic data when raw data cannot be pooled; the IDS is trained exclusively on synthetic records (5,000 rows per class);
    \item \textbf{TAug (Train Augmented, Test Real):} Assesses whether synthetic data adds value when real data is available; the real training data is augmented with synthetic records until each class contains 20,000 rows;
    \item \textbf{TAugR (Train Augmented Rare, Test Real):} Injects synthetic records only into the rare classes, at an augmentation ratio selected on the validation set;
    \item \textbf{Local Augmentation:} Simulates gateways improving their own IDS; each client supplements its real data with synthetic records from the global model only for the classes it lacks (at most 5,000 rows per class), yielding 15 client-specific models (5 clients $\times$ 3 seeds);
    \item \textbf{IDS-Side Options:} Examines whether the IDS can compensate for data limitations by using the full 20,000 synthetic rows per class together with class-probability weights tuned on the validation set;
    \item \textbf{Direct FedMLP:} Non-generative reference in which a classifier is trained directly with FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017}, optionally under DP and SecAgg$^+$, without an intermediate synthetic data step.
\end{itemize}
Two downstream classifiers are implemented with scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011}: a Random Forest~\cite{breiman2001} with 200 trees, and an MLP with two hidden layers of 128 and 64 units, early stopping, and at most 100 training iterations.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Metrics}
The performance, privacy, and system cost of each configuration are measured along the five dimensions summarized in Table~\ref{tab:metrics}. Macro-F1 serves as the primary utility metric. Every membership inference attack (MIA) is accompanied by a positive control, such as a deliberately overfitted model, to confirm that the attack is functional; an AUC close to 0.5 is uninformative if the positive control also fails to detect membership~\cite{carlini2022}.


\subsubsection{Statistical Decision Rule}
To decide whether a configuration $A$ is genuinely superior to a baseline configuration $B$, a dual-condition decision rule is applied instead of comparing point estimates. Let $S$ denote the number of random seeds and $F_{A,s}$ the primary utility metric (macro-F1) of configuration $A$ at seed $s$. The mean paired difference across seeds is
\begin{equation}
\bar{\Delta} = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\left(F_{A,s} - F_{B,s}\right).
\label{eq:mean_diff}
\end{equation}
Configuration $A$ is deemed to have a significant and practically relevant effect over $B$, denoted $A \succ B$, if and only if the 95\% confidence interval of the paired bootstrap mean difference excludes zero and the absolute mean difference exceeds the larger seed-to-seed standard deviation of the two configurations:
\begin{equation}
A \succ B \iff 0 \notin \mathrm{CI}_{95\%}(\bar{\Delta}) \land |\bar{\Delta}| > \max(\mathrm{sd}_A, \mathrm{sd}_B).
\label{eq:decision_rule}
\end{equation}
This study does not report $p$-values and applies no multiple-testing correction; all conclusions rely solely on rule~\eqref{eq:decision_rule}.

\subsubsection{Stream-Level Sensitivity Check}
Because packets belonging to the same TCP stream are not independent and identically distributed, the bootstrap procedure is repeated with resampling at the TCP stream level instead of the row level. Any configuration for which the stream-level bootstrap changes the outcome of rule~\eqref{eq:decision_rule} is explicitly flagged in the analysis of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Methodological Limitations}\label{subsec:limitations}
The empirical conclusions of this study are bounded by the following methodological constraints:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data and Granularity:} Evaluated on a single IoT dataset (DoS/DDoS-MQTT-IoT~\cite{alatram2023}) at the packet level. Inter-packet temporal correlations across extended sessions are not modeled. The Normal subset was re-exported from the original PCAP files and additionally contains broker-originated packets that the authors' export settings exclude (Section~\ref{subsubsec:provenance});
    \item \textbf{Privacy Guarantees:} Guarantees apply at the packet level by default. Stream-level guarantees assume strict participation ceilings. Normalization statistics and hyperparameter tuning are conducted centrally on validation data outside the DP budget;
    \item \textbf{Generative Expressiveness:} FedDP-Marginal relies on first-order tree factorizations; second-order structures (PrivBayes) represent an extension. The deep route evaluates a single CVAE topology;
    \item \textbf{Statistical Power:} Multi-client simulations utilize three random seeds $\{0, 1, 2\}$, which capture variance but do not permit high-order asymptotic claims;
    \item \textbf{Physical Simulation:} Network latency, packet dropouts, and hardware-constrained edge battery depletion are simulated within a shared CPU environment rather than evaluated on physical microcontrollers.
\end{itemize}
