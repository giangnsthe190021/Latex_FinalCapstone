\chapter{Methodology}
Describes the research design, data collection methods, and analysis techniques.

\section{Research Design}

\subsection{Research Type and Design Overview}
This study is a quantitative, controlled experimental study carried out on one published network-traffic dataset, DoS/DDoS-MQTT-IoT~\cite{alatram2023}. In the terminology of the FPT thesis guide, it combines Experiments with Archival Research: the independent variables (generator, privacy mechanism, privacy budget) are manipulated by the researchers, while the traffic itself is existing recorded data rather than data collected specifically for this study.

\subsubsection*{Research Object}
The object of study is a framework, PP-FedData, that lets several IoT/MQTT gateways jointly produce synthetic network-traffic records without any gateway releasing its raw packets, so that an intrusion detection system (IDS) can be trained on data that no single party could pool. The framework is evaluated along four dimensions: the utility of the synthetic data for the IDS, the privacy guarantee attached to the released artefacts, the communication and computation overhead, and the fit of each configuration to a stated deployment requirement.

\subsubsection*{Experimental Control}
Internal validity rests on holding everything except the studied factors fixed. Every configuration in the experiment matrix (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_configs}) is run with:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the same train/validation/test split, formed by capture group, with a real test split that is locked and never used for tuning (train 88,500, validation 12,000, test 30,000 rows; class quotas fixed in the configuration);
    \item the same non-IID partition of the training rows over $K = 5$ clients, drawn from a Dirichlet distribution with concentration $\alpha = 0.5$~\cite{hsu2019} and fixed by the seed;
    \item the same preprocessing ($\log(1+x)$ transformation of skewed columns, standardisation, clipping at $\pm 5\sigma$, one-hot encoding of categorical columns), which turns the harmonised packet attributes into 60 model input columns;
    \item the same downstream classifiers: a Random Forest~\cite{breiman2001} with 200 trees and a multilayer perceptron with hidden layers $[128, 64]$;
    \item the same three random seeds $\{0, 1, 2\}$;
    \item the same real test split for every reported score.
\end{itemize}

Under this design, differences between configurations are attributable to the generator and to the protection mechanism, subject to the limits listed in Section~\ref{sec:methodology_limitations}. The design does not claim that extraneous influences are eliminated: hyper-parameter selection, the choice of capture groups, and the number of seeds all remain sources of variation, and Section~\ref{sec:methodology_limitations} states how far each is covered.

\subsubsection*{Evolution of the Design}
The study was originally designed around a federated conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE)~\cite{sohn2015cvae}, which is the premise of the project. After a first evaluation round, the framework adopted a marginal-based generator, FedDP-Marginal, as its primary generator and retained the CVAE as a baseline. The decision is recorded with its date and rationale in the project's deviation log (\texttt{docs/SPEC\_DEVIATIONS.md}, entries O1.9 and O2.3); Chapter~4 presents the evidence and Chapter~5 discusses it. The CVAE configurations remain in the matrix so that the comparison in RQ2 is made under identical federation, privacy, and secure-aggregation conditions.

\subsection{System Architecture and the Two Generator Routes}

\subsubsection*{Roles}
The system involves three primary roles (Figure~\ref{fig:system_arch}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Gateways (federated learning clients):} The main study uses $K = 5$ gateways. Each gateway holds the traffic it observed and is trusted with its own data. Because the partition is non-IID, some gateways have few or no examples of some attack classes.
    \item \textbf{Aggregation server:} The server coordinates the rounds, combines client contributions and, after training or table release, produces the synthetic data. It is modelled as honest-but-curious (Section~\ref{subsec:threat_model}).
    \item \textbf{Downstream IDS:} A Random Forest and a multilayer perceptron are trained on synthetic data (TSTR) or on real plus synthetic data (TAug, TAugR, local augmentation) and are evaluated on the real test split.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Where Synthetic Data is Generated}
Synthetic records are generated at the server, from the global model (CVAE decoder) or from the released global tables (marginal counts and dependency tree), by sampling conditionally on the class label. They are not generated at the gateways. The sampling functions are implemented in \texttt{models/generate.py} (CVAE) and \texttt{models/marginal.py} (FedDP-Marginal).

\subsubsection*{What the Server Sees}
The visibility of client contributions depends on the configuration and must not be stated in general terms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item M2, M3, MGs, and MGr use Flower's SecAgg$^+$ protocol~\cite{bell2020, beutel2020}: the server receives masked vectors and can recover only their sum.
    \item B3 and M1 use plain FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017}: the server sees each client's model update; in M1 the update already carries noise added by DP-SGD.
    \item MGl (local DP): each client adds the full noise to its own tables and sends them without secure aggregation, so the server sees each client's noisy tables.
    \item MGd and MGb simulate the distributed sum inside one process; they do not pass through the SecAgg$^+$ protocol.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{The Two Generator Routes}
Table~\ref{tab:two_routes} contrasts the two routes. FedDP-Marginal exchanges counts of discrete attribute combinations in three releases; the CVAE exchanges continuous model weights over many FedAvg rounds. The difference matters for privacy accounting. Each marginal release is a single additive aggregate with bounded sensitivity, so the calibrated noise can be divided among the clients and the sum computed through secure aggregation. DP-SGD~\cite{abadi2016dpsgd}, by contrast, performs many local non-linear steps per round, and noise added by one client passes through the other clients' training before aggregation; the project therefore does not claim distributed DP for the CVAE route (\texttt{SPEC\_DEVIATIONS.md}, O2.1).

\subsection{Threat Model, Trust Assumptions and Privacy Units}\label{subsec:threat_model}

\subsubsection*{Adversary and Trust Assumptions}
The aggregation server is assumed to be honest-but-curious, also called semi-honest~\cite{goldreich2004}: it follows the protocol but may analyse every message it receives. Gateways hold their own traffic and are trusted with it. The study considers four trust settings, which differ in how much the privacy guarantee depends on other gateways:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Base setting:} All $K$ gateways follow the protocol and each adds its designated share of the noise ($1/K$ of the variance in the distributed variants).
    \item \textbf{$h$ honest gateways:} The guarantee is also reported when only $h \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ gateways add their share; the remaining gateways may collude with the server or drop out. The reported $\varepsilon$ then grows as $h$ decreases. For the distributed FedDP-Marginal variant, the project reports $\varepsilon$ for every $h$; Chapter~4 gives the values.
    \item \textbf{Threshold $t$:} The per-client noise is calibrated so that the target $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ holds whenever at least $t$ gateways contribute their share. Each client then adds $1/t$ of the calibrated variance. The default is $t = K$.
    \item \textbf{Local DP (MGl):} Each gateway adds the full noise itself, so no other gateway needs to be trusted~\cite{kasiviswanathan2011}; the total noise variance in the aggregate grows with the number of clients.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Out of Scope}
Active attacks by malicious gateways, namely data poisoning, model poisoning, and Byzantine behaviour, are outside the threat model. Statements in this thesis about privacy do not extend to them.

\subsubsection*{Roles of Secure Aggregation and Differential Privacy}
The two mechanisms address different threats and are not interchangeable (Table~\ref{tab:protection_mechanisms}).

\subsubsection*{Privacy Unit}
The default unit is the record, that is, one network packet. An $\varepsilon$ at record level protects the presence of one packet. For FedDP-Marginal, two larger units are available: the TCP stream (suffix \texttt{-strm\{m\}}) and the capture (suffix \texttt{-cap\{m\}}). Their guarantee holds only under two conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item each unit is held entirely by one client (the partition is made by unit, not by row);
    \item each client uses at most $m$ rows of each unit.
\end{enumerate}
When both hold, a release of $T$ tables has sensitivities $\Delta_1 = m \cdot T$ in $\ell_1$ and $\Delta_2 = m \sqrt{T}$ in $\ell_2$ (\texttt{group\_dp.py}), and the noise is calibrated to these values. The CVAE routes keep record-level $\varepsilon$.

\subsubsection*{Why the Unit Must Be Stated}
Packets of one stream, and packets of one capture, are correlated. Under record-level $\varepsilon$, a set of $k$ correlated records from the same source is protected only through group privacy, which weakens the guarantee to roughly $k\varepsilon$ with a correspondingly larger $\delta$~\cite{dwork2014}. A statement of $\varepsilon$ without its unit therefore says little about whether an attack session as a whole is concealed. The unit statistics of the data are: the training split contains 76,463 TCP streams and 147 capture groups, and a capture contains between 62 and 1,277 rows.

\subsubsection*{Public Side Information (Not Covered by $\varepsilon$)}
The following are treated as known to every party and are outside the accounting:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the class labels and the number of examples per class;
    \item client sizes $n_i$, where they are transmitted in clear (Flower's \texttt{num\_examples} in the CVAE routes);
    \item the normalisation statistics, the $\log(1+x)$ choices, the category lists, and the mask of unused categories, computed centrally on the pooled training split before partitioning;
    \item the hyper-parameter selection, performed on the real validation split.
\end{itemize}
In the FedDP-Marginal route the client reports \texttt{num\_examples} as a fixed public constant, so client sizes do not travel through that field (\texttt{SPEC\_DEVIATIONS.md}, O2.3).

\subsubsection*{Standard Formulation for the Thesis}
We assume an honest-but-curious aggregation server that follows the protocol but may analyse every message it receives. Gateways hold their own traffic and are trusted with it. In the base setting every gateway follows the protocol and adds its share of the noise; we also report the guarantee when only $h$ of the $K$ gateways do so, and an optional threshold $t$ calibrates the noise so that the target $\varepsilon$ holds whenever at least $t$ gateways add their share (the others may collude with the server or drop out). In the local-DP variant each gateway adds the full noise, so no other gateway needs to be trusted. Malicious (poisoning or Byzantine) gateways are out of scope.

Secure aggregation hides each gateway's contribution from the server, which learns only the sum; it does not limit what the aggregate reveals and receives no privacy credit. Differential privacy bounds what the released tables, model, or synthetic data reveal about one record (one packet) with $\delta = 10^{-5}$. Class labels, class counts, and client sizes are treated as public side information and are not protected.

\subsection{Requirement-Driven Recommendation Rule}\label{subsec:req_rule}

\subsubsection*{Motivation and Relation to Rule R6}
The original specification fixed a single rule, R6: among the configurations whose membership-inference AUC is at most 0.55, whose $\varepsilon$ (maximum over clients and worst seed) is at most 5 when DP is used, and whose time per round is at most three times that of B3, choose the one with the highest TAug macro-F1 of the Random Forest. Ties within the seed noise are broken by the strength of the formal protection. The thresholds are heuristics fixed by the research team (Section~\ref{sec:methodology_limitations}). R6 is retained as a reference rule. A single fixed criterion cannot express deployments that differ in trust, bandwidth, and privacy unit, so the framework adds a requirement-driven rule, implemented as \texttt{ppfeddata recommend} (\texttt{recommend.py}), which takes a deployment requirement as input.

\subsubsection*{Requirement Formulation}
A requirement is the tuple:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{R} = \langle & \text{Trust}_{\text{server}}, \text{Trust}_{\text{clients}}, \varepsilon_{\max}, \text{BW}_{\text{round}}, \text{BW}_{\text{total}}, \\
& \text{Rounds}_{\max}, \text{Unit}, \text{Priority}, \text{Recall}_{\text{rare},\min}, \text{MIA}_{\max}, h \rangle
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\text{Trust}_{\text{server}}$ and $\text{Trust}_{\text{clients}}$ state whether the server and the other clients are trusted; $\varepsilon_{\max}$ is the record-level (or unit-level) budget, or none if no DP is required; $\text{BW}_{\text{round}}$, $\text{BW}_{\text{total}}$, and $\text{Rounds}_{\max}$ bound the gateway bandwidth per round, over the whole run, and in rounds; $\text{Unit} \in \{\text{packet}, \text{stream}, \text{capture}\}$ is the unit $\varepsilon$ must cover; $\text{Priority} \in \{\text{macro}, \text{binary}, \text{rare}\}$ selects TSTR macro-F1, binary F1, or mean rare-class recall; $\text{Recall}_{\text{rare},\min}$ is a floor on the rare-class recall; $\text{MIA}_{\max}$ is the maximum acceptable membership-inference AUC; and $h$ is the number of clients assumed to add their noise share.

\subsubsection*{Procedure}
Inputs are the rows of the scorecard, which are produced from the run ledger; no figure is typed by hand. The rule applies five steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Validity filter:} A row is considered only if its reported quantities correspond to what its name claims. For example, a DP configuration with a component computed from data outside the $\varepsilon$ accounting (residual noise computed from real training data) is not valid; a directly federated classifier is a reference and is excluded from the generator front.
    \item \textbf{Trust filter:} If the server is not trusted, only rows with secure aggregation or local DP remain. If clients may collude, the $\varepsilon$ used for a distributed-DP row is the one that holds with the assumed number of honest clients (one honest client by default, or $h$ when it is specified); local-DP rows keep their $\varepsilon$ because each client adds the full noise.
    \item \textbf{Constraint filter:} Rows that exceed the $\varepsilon$ budget (with a 2\% tolerance), the per-round or total bandwidth, the number of rounds, or the MIA bound, that do not protect the requested unit (a row protecting a packet does not satisfy a stream or capture requirement), or whose rare-class recall is below the floor, are removed. Missing cost figures do not exclude a row; they are reported as not available.
    \item \textbf{Pareto front:} Among the remaining rows, configuration A dominates B if A is at least as good on every axis and better on at least one. The axes are the three utility measures (TSTR macro-F1, binary F1, rare-class recall), $\varepsilon$ under the assumed trust, the presence of secure aggregation, MB per round, and total MB. A utility difference counts only if it exceeds the seed standard deviation of both configurations; $\varepsilon$ values within 2\% and cost values within 5\% are treated as equal; a configuration with secure aggregation is preferred to one without when the other axes are equal.
    \item \textbf{Selection and ties:} The configuration with the best value on the requested priority is selected, and every configuration within the seed standard deviation of it is listed as tied.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Label Harmonisation and Rare Classes}\label{subsec:labels}
The labels are harmonised into a six-class mode: NORMAL, BCF, DELAYED, SYN, INVALID, and WILL. In this mode, the DoS and DDoS variants of the same attack family are merged into one class. The merge is supported by leakage check C5 (Section~\ref{subsec:leakage_checks}), which shows that DoS and DDoS variants of the same family are practically indistinguishable at packet level. The original eleven-class mode remains available in the pipeline but is used only for the robustness branch (Table~\ref{tab:robustness_branches}).

In this study, the rare classes are the attack classes whose training quota is at most 5,000 rows, namely DELAYED, SYN, INVALID, and WILL. Their rarity in training is a deliberate consequence of the quota design rather than a lack of raw data: as Table~\ref{tab:dataset_composition} shows, the raw dataset contains between 270,529 and 601,542 packets for each of them.

The quotas create an imbalance of 60:1 between the majority class (NORMAL, 60,000 rows) and the smallest class (WILL, 1,000 rows), which makes the evaluation of the generators demanding~\cite{he2009}. The validation and test splits, in contrast, are class-balanced (2,000 and 5,000 rows per class respectively), so that the evaluation measures are not dominated by the majority class.

\subsection{Schema Harmonisation and Feature Selection}\label{subsec:schema}
Preprocessing starts by harmonising the schema of the raw packet exports. Column names are converted to snake case. Wireshark text labels (such as \texttt{message\_type}, \texttt{qos\_level\_*}, and \texttt{requested\_qos}) are normalised to canonical codes, and boolean indicators (True/False, Set/Not set) are mapped to 0/1. Redundant columns with the suffix \texttt{\_1} are removed. For multi-valued cells that contain several MQTT messages, only the first element is kept, in line with the Normal export, which used the TShark option \texttt{occurrence=f}; the \texttt{n\_mqtt\_msgs} column is therefore dropped. Rows are then restricted to those whose protocol is TCP or MQTT, followed by a format audit of the time-based tokens.

To prevent leakage~\cite{kaufman2012, arp2022}, identifier and metadata columns are removed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{no} (row sequence), \texttt{epoch\_time} (absolute timestamp), and \texttt{time\_since\_reference\_\allowbreak or\_first\_frame} (which reveals the start time of the capture);
    \item \texttt{source}, because sender IP addresses would let a model memorise machine identities instead of learning traffic patterns;
    \item the free-text payload in \texttt{info}, and the \texttt{label} and \texttt{capture\_id} columns, which exist only in the Normal traffic.
\end{itemize}
\texttt{stream\_index} is kept only as grouping metadata and is not an input feature. The raw dataset contains no MAC address or port columns. After the feature selection decisions, the final schema has $D = 60$ input columns (Table~\ref{tab:feature_schema}).

\subsection{Group-Based Split and Quota Sampling}\label{subsec:split}
The eleven sub-classes (NORMAL and the DoS and DDoS variants of the five attack families) are split by group: whole capture groups are assigned to the training, validation, or test split according to a fixed \texttt{split\_seed}. This keeps highly correlated packets of the same TCP stream or capture session from appearing in more than one split~\cite{arp2022}.

The source data limits this approach: 8 of the 11 sub-classes come from a single capture file. For these sub-classes, the validation and test rows are taken as consecutive blocks of the same capture used for training (block split), separated by buffers of 1,000 rows to reduce adjacency leakage. TCP streams that straddle a block boundary are dropped entirely. This removes between 1.2\% and 9.9\% of the eligible rows of each affected sub-class and biases the sample slightly toward shorter connections.

\subsection{Preprocessing and Encoding}\label{subsec:preprocessing}
The preprocessing pipeline is fitted on the training split only; the validation and test splits are transformed with the stored parameters. Missing or non-applicable numeric values are imputed with 0, and an indicator column \texttt{\_is\_na} is added for every feature whose missing rate in the training split lies strictly between 0.005 and 0.995.

Numeric features with a skewness greater than 2 are transformed with $\log(1+x)$; time-delta features are first multiplied by 1,000 (seconds to milliseconds). Numeric columns are then standardised to zero mean and unit variance and clipped at $\pm 5\sigma$ to limit the influence of extreme values. Categorical features are one-hot encoded. To bound the dimension, only the ten most frequent training tokens of each categorical column are kept; other valid values are grouped into an \texttt{OTHER} token and missing values are mapped to a \texttt{NONE} token.

The normalisation statistics, category lists, and imputation parameters are fitted centrally on the pooled training split. This is a limitation of the method: it assumes a centralised view of the data before federation, and these statistics lie outside the privacy budget $\varepsilon$ (Section~\ref{subsec:threat_model}).

\section{Sampling Data Analysis Techniques}

\subsection{Federated Partitioning}\label{subsec:partitioning}

\subsubsection*{Dirichlet Partition of the Training Data}
To model non-identically distributed (non-IID) traffic across edge gateways, the training split is partitioned over a federation of gateways $\mathcal{K} = \{1, 2, \dots, K\}$, with $K = 5$ in the main study. The partition follows the label-skew model of Hsu et~al.~\cite{hsu2019}, which uses a symmetric Dirichlet distribution with concentration parameter $\alpha_{\text{dir}}$. For each class $c \in \{1, \dots, C\}$ ($C = 6$ in the six-class mode), the proportions assigned to the $K$ gateways are drawn independently:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_c = (p_{c,1}, p_{c,2}, \dots, p_{c,K}) \sim \mathrm{Dir}(\alpha_{\text{dir}} \mathbf{1}_K), \qquad \mathbf{1}_K = (1, \dots, 1) \in \mathbb{R}^K.
\label{eq:dirichlet}
\end{equation}
The concentration parameter controls the heterogeneity between gateways~\cite{hsu2019}: small values ($\alpha_{\text{dir}} \to 0$) concentrate each class on few gateways, while large values ($\alpha_{\text{dir}} \to \infty$) approach an IID allocation. The main study uses $\alpha_{\text{dir}} = 0.5$. Under this allocation, some gateways receive few or no examples of the rare attack classes.

\subsubsection*{Record-Level and Unit-Level Partition}
Two partition modes are distinguished:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Record-level partition (default):} Each packet record $(x, y)$ of class $c$ is assigned to gateway $k$ with probability $p_{c,k}$. Packets of the same TCP stream or capture group can therefore reach several gateways. This models an open edge network in which the packets of one flow pass through different regional routers.
    \item \textbf{Unit-level partition (group DP):} Used only for the stream- and capture-level privacy evaluations (Section~\ref{subsec:privacy_accounting}). Capture groups or TCP streams are treated as indivisible units: the Dirichlet proportions are applied to the majority class of each unit, and all records of a unit go to one gateway. No protected unit is split across gateways, which gives a clear boundary for bounding each unit's contribution.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Constraints and Safeguards}
The partition enforces the following constraints:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Resampling floor:} If any gateway receives fewer than 500 records in total ($\sum_{c} n_{k,c} < 500$), the proportions are rejected and drawn again from $\mathrm{Dir}(\alpha_{\text{dir}} \mathbf{1}_K)$ until every gateway reaches the floor.
    \item \textbf{Unit-level floor:} In the unit-level partition, each gateway must hold at least 200 rows, so that every gateway has enough local samples for aggregation.
    \item \textbf{One partition per seed:} Each seed $s \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ draws an independent partition $\mathbf{p}_c^{(s)}$, and every model evaluated with seed $s$ uses that same partition, so the partition does not confound the comparison of models.
    \item \textbf{Split before partition:} The global train/validation/test split is made before the allocation to gateways, so the validation and test rows never enter the federated training pool. Full session independence between splits is still limited by the data: for 8 of the 11 sub-classes, the validation and test rows are consecutive blocks of the capture used for training (Section~\ref{subsec:split} and Section~\ref{sec:methodology_limitations}).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Invariance of the Summed Marginals to the Partition}
For any count query summed through SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020}, the global result is the exact, unweighted sum of the local integer count vectors:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{global}} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbf{h}_k = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_k} \mathbf{e}(x_i) = \sum_{i \in \bigcup_{k} \mathcal{D}_k} \mathbf{e}(x_i),
\label{eq:hist_sum}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}(x_i)$ is the indicator vector of the bin that contains record $x_i$. Because integer addition is commutative and associative, the summed table without noise is identical to the table computed centrally, whatever the label skew $P_k(Y)$ or covariate shift $P_k(X \mid Y)$.

Running FedDP-Marginal with $\alpha_{\text{dir}} \in \{0.1, 0.5, 10\}$ is therefore a null prediction, or sanity check: without client dropout or unit capping, its summed marginals and synthesis are in theory unaffected by the partition skew. In a real federation, heterogeneity affects FedDP-Marginal only in two cases:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Client dropout:} The counts of a dropped gateway are missing from the sum, and under small $\alpha_{\text{dir}}$ that gateway may hold class samples that no surviving gateway has.
    \item \textbf{Record-level partition with a unit cap $m$:} A unit spread over several gateways is capped at $m$ rows by each of them, whereas on a single gateway it would be capped only once.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{FedDP-Marginal}\label{subsec:feddp_marginal}

\subsubsection*{Positioning and Overview}
FedDP-Marginal adapts private marginal-based synthesis of tabular data, namely the MST method of McKenna et~al.~\cite{mckenna2021mst} and the graphical-model principles of PrivBayes~\cite{zhang2017privbayes}, to a federated setting. It is not a new generative method; it adapts marginal-based dependency modelling to federation through secure integer aggregation. Unlike deep federated models, which need many rounds of weight exchange, FedDP-Marginal uses three query releases coordinated through SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020} in Flower~\cite{beutel2020} (Figure~\ref{fig:three_release}).

\subsubsection*{Attribute Schema ($d = 37$) and One-Hot Representation ($D = 60$)}
The marginal generator and the neural generator represent features differently:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tabular schema ($d = 37$ attributes):} FedDP-Marginal queries count tables over 37 discrete or discretised attributes:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item 13 numeric attributes (for example inter-arrival times, segment lengths, TCP window sizes), discretised into $B_{\text{fine}} = 64$ uniform bins over the normalised interval $[-5, 5]$, which matches the $\pm 5\sigma$ clipping of preprocessing;
        \item 19 binary attributes: 7 protocol flags (\texttt{syn}, \texttt{reset}, \texttt{acknowledgment}, \texttt{retain}, \texttt{clean\_session\_flag\_2}, \texttt{will\_retain}, \texttt{will\_flag}) and 12 \texttt{\_is\_na} indicators;
        \item 5 categorical attributes (\texttt{message\_type}, \texttt{qos\_level\_1}, \texttt{qos\_level\_2}, \texttt{requested\_qos}, \texttt{protocol}), kept as discrete variables in the tables.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{One-hot representation ($D = 60$):} The CVAE baseline works on $\mathbb{R}^{D}$ with $D = 60$, obtained by expanding the 5 categorical attributes into 28 one-hot columns ($13 + 19 + 28 = 60$; Table~\ref{tab:feature_schema}).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Three-Release Protocol}
The protocol issues three releases in sequence and divides the privacy budget between them:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Release 1 (fine univariate marginals per class):} Each gateway counts, for every attribute $X_i$ ($i = 1, \dots, d$) and class $c$, the rows in each of the $B_{\text{fine}} = 64$ fine bins, adds its share of discrete noise, and sends the result through SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020}. From the summed noisy histograms, the server sets data-dependent coarse cut-points for the numeric attributes at empirical quantiles, with $B_{\text{coarse}} \in \{4, 8, 16, 32\}$ (default 16). The coarse cut-points coincide with edges of the fine bins.
    \item \textbf{Release 2 (coarse pairwise marginals, pooled over classes):} Gateways count all $\binom{d}{2} = 666$ attribute pairs $(X_i, X_j)$ over the coarse bins, pooled over all classes. Pooling maximises the count per cell and bounds the table size to $B_{\text{coarse}}^2$, which limits the effect of the privacy noise on dependency estimation.
    \item \textbf{Release 3 (tree-edge marginals per class):} Using the structure derived from Release~2, the server requests the joint tables of the $d - 1$ edges of the global Chow--Liu tree, conditioned on the class $y = c$, over the coarse bins (at most $B_{\text{coarse}}^2 \cdot C$ cells, so that the noise does not swamp the rare classes), together with the class-conditional table of the root attribute over the fine bins.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{One Global Chow--Liu Tree}
FedDP-Marginal builds a single global Chow--Liu dependency tree~\cite{chow1968}, pooled over all classes, rather than one tree per class. From the post-processed tables of Release~2, the server computes the empirical mutual information (MI) between attributes and extracts the maximum spanning tree $T^\star$ with Kruskal's algorithm~\cite{kruskal1956}:
\begin{equation}
T^\star = \arg\max_{T} \sum_{(i,j) \in T} \hat{I}(X_i; X_j), \qquad \hat{I}(X_i; X_j) = \sum_{a, b} \hat{p}(a, b) \log \frac{\hat{p}(a, b)}{\hat{p}(a)\, \hat{p}(b)}.
\label{eq:chow_liu}
\end{equation}
Following the post-processing of McKenna et~al.~\cite{mckenna2021mst}, the probabilities are obtained from the noisy pairwise counts $c_{i,j}$ by truncation at zero, additive (Laplace) smoothing $\alpha_{\text{smooth}}$, and normalisation:
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}(a, b) = \frac{\max(0, c_{i,j}(a, b)) + \alpha_{\text{smooth}}}{\sum_{a', b'} \left[\max(0, c_{i,j}(a', b')) + \alpha_{\text{smooth}}\right]}.
\label{eq:phat}
\end{equation}
The univariate terms $\hat{p}(a)$ and $\hat{p}(b)$ are obtained by summing out the other dimension. By the post-processing property of differential privacy~\cite{dwork2014}, deriving $T^\star$ spends no additional privacy budget.

\textbf{Trade-off of pooling.} The class-conditional MI $\hat{I}(X_i; X_j \mid Y = c)$ would match class-conditional synthesis more closely. However, splitting Release~2 by class leaves the sensitivity per cell unchanged while dividing the signal per cell by about $C$, and the total noise over the $C\binom{d}{2}$ tables grows $C$-fold. The signal-to-noise ratio of each cell therefore drops by a factor of $C$, which corrupts the choice of edges. FedDP-Marginal deliberately pools the pairwise counts over classes to maximise the signal-to-noise ratio of the skeleton $T^\star$. The pooled MI then reflects the overall dependency structure, which is dominated by NORMAL traffic (67.8\% of the training quota); class-specific relations are captured afterwards by the class-conditional edge tables of Release~3.

\textbf{Cardinality bias.} The plug-in MI on noisy tables has a finite-sample bias proportional to the degrees of freedom of the table, $(|X_i| - 1)(|X_j| - 1)$. Numeric attributes have a higher coarse cardinality ($B_{\text{coarse}} = 16$) than binary flags ($|\cdot| = 2$), so a pair of numeric attributes has up to $(16 - 1)^2 = 225$ degrees of freedom against $(2 - 1)^2 = 1$ for a pair of flags. Kruskal's algorithm on unnormalised plug-in MI therefore tends to favour edges between high-cardinality numeric attributes. Laplace smoothing adds a uniform pseudo-mass $\alpha_{\text{smooth}} |X_i||X_j|$, which dilutes the probabilities more in larger tables. The Miller--Madow correction~\cite{miller1955}
\begin{equation}
\hat{I}_{\text{MM}}(X_i; X_j) = \hat{I}(X_i; X_j) - \frac{(|X_i| - 1)(|X_j| - 1)}{2\hat{N}},
\label{eq:miller_madow}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{N} = \sum_{a,b} \max(0, c_{i,j}(a,b))$ is the total truncated count, corrects the asymptotic multinomial sampling bias but not the additive Skellam noise or the non-linear truncation $\max(0, \cdot)$. The main pipeline keeps the uncorrected plug-in MI with coarse bins ($B_{\text{coarse}} \le 16$) as the baseline heuristic. To measure the effect of the cardinality bias on the structure, separately from downstream utility, search space~(d) (Section~\ref{subsec:hpo}) includes an ablation that compares plug-in MI, Miller--Madow MI, and normalised MI ($\hat{I}(X_i; X_j)/\sqrt{H(X_i) H(X_j)}$), together with a check of the stability of the tree edges across partition seeds.

\subsubsection*{Class-Conditional Sampling}
Synthetic records of class $c$ are generated by traversing $T^\star$ from the root to the leaves~\cite{chow1968}:
\begin{equation}
\hat{P}(\mathbf{x} \mid y = c) = \hat{P}(x_r \mid y = c) \prod_{(u,v) \in T^\star} \hat{P}(x_v \mid x_u, y = c),
\label{eq:tree_factorisation}
\end{equation}
where $r$ is the root attribute. The steps are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Root selection and sampling:} For MGs, the root is the attribute with the largest sum of noisy MI, $\sum_{j \ne r} \hat{I}(X_r; X_j)$. It is sampled from its class-conditional fine distribution of Release~1, $x_r \sim \hat{P}(X_r \mid y = c)$.
    \item \textbf{Conditional propagation:} Each child is sampled given its parent, $x_v \sim \hat{P}(X_v \mid X_u = x_u, y = c)$. The noisy counts are truncated at zero and smoothed with $\alpha_{\text{smooth}}$.
    \item \textbf{Refinement of numeric attributes:} The sampled coarse bin is refined to a fine bin in proportion to the fine class histogram of Release~1 (whose fine bins nest inside the coarse bins), and a value is drawn uniformly within the fine bin $[b_{\text{low}}, b_{\text{high}}]$.
    \item \textbf{Inverse transformation:} The vectors pass through the same inverse pipeline as the CVAE baseline (inverse standardisation and inverse $\log(1+x)$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{Sensitivity, Budget Split and Consistency Refinement (MGr)}
\textbf{Neighbouring relation and sensitivity.} The accounting uses unbounded adjacency: two datasets $D \sim D'$ differ by the addition or removal of one packet record. One record increments exactly one cell in each of the $d$ tables of Release~1, the $\binom{d}{2}$ tables of Release~2, and the $d$ tables of Release~3 ($d - 1$ edge tables and one root table). For a release of $T$ tables, the global sensitivities are $\Delta_1 = T$ and $\Delta_2 = \sqrt{T}$. Under bounded DP (replacement of a record within a class), they become $\Delta_1^{\text{repl}} = 2T$ and $\Delta_2^{\text{repl}} = \sqrt{2T}$.

\textbf{Budget split.} The budget is divided between releases by a split vector $\mathbf{s} = (s_1, s_2, s_3)$ with $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 1$, by default $(0.3, 0.1, 0.6)$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For the Gaussian variant (MGd), the zCDP parameter is split as $\rho_r = s_r \rho$, which gives the noise standard deviation $\sigma_r = \Delta_{2,r}/\sqrt{2 s_r \rho}$ for release $r$.
    \item For the Skellam variant (MGs), the noise parameter of release $r$ is $\mu_{\text{sk},r} = \Delta_{2,r}^2/(2 s_r \kappa)$, where the global factor $\kappa > 0$ is found by binary search.
    \item With the default split, Release~2 has $\Delta_{2,2}^2 = \binom{37}{2} = 666$ with $s_2 = 0.1$, and Release~1 has $\Delta_{2,1}^2 = 37$ with $s_1 = 0.3$, so $\mu_{\text{sk},2}/\mu_{\text{sk},1} = (666/0.1)/(37/0.3) = 18 \times 3 = 54$. Release~2 thus adds about 54 times the noise variance per cell of Release~1, which makes coarse binning ($B_{\text{coarse}} \le 16$) necessary to keep a usable signal-to-noise ratio.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Consistency refinement (MGr).} To make the tables consistent without spending budget, MGr applies the following post-processing:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bin-wise minimum-variance merging:} Merging is done on counts, per class, before normalisation. The fine counts of Release~1 are collapsed to coarse bins; a coarse bin $b$ of $X_u$ that contains $k_b$ fine bins has variance $k_b \sigma_1^2$. Projecting an incident Release~3 edge table $(X_u, X_v)$ onto $X_u$, by summing over the $|X_v|$ coarse bins of $X_v$, gives an independent estimate with variance $|X_v| \sigma_3^2$. A non-root attribute with degree $d(u)$ in $T^\star$ therefore has $1 + d(u)$ independent estimators. For the root $r$, Release~3 also provides the class-conditional fine root table, a second univariate estimator with variance $k_b \sigma_3^2$, so the root merges $2 + d(r)$ estimators. Following McKenna et~al.~\cite{mckenna2021mst}, the estimators are merged bin by bin with inverse-variance weights:
    \begin{equation}
    \hat{C}_{\text{merged}}(X_u = b \mid y = c) = \frac{\sum_{j} w_{u,j}(b)\, \hat{C}_{u,j}(b \mid y = c)}{\sum_{j} w_{u,j}(b)},
    \label{eq:ivw_merge}
    \end{equation}
    with weights $w_{u,j}(b) = 1/\mathrm{Var}\big(\hat{C}_{u,j}(b \mid y = c)\big)$. The merged counts are then normalised, $\hat{P}_{\text{merged}}(b) = \hat{C}_{\text{merged}}(b)/\sum_{b'} \hat{C}_{\text{merged}}(b')$.
    \item \textbf{Truncation:} The merged marginals and the noisy Release~3 tables are truncated at zero and normalised to sum to 1.
    \item \textbf{Iterative proportional fitting (IPF):} Each edge table $(X_u, X_v \mid y = c)$ is adjusted by IPF~\cite{deming1940, csiszar1975} to match the merged marginals of $X_u$ and $X_v$, which makes the margins consistent.
    \item \textbf{Smoothing and root selection:} Laplace smoothing $\alpha_{\text{smooth}} \in \{0.1, 1, 10\}$ is applied, and the root is the attribute with the largest sum of noisy MI.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Second-order Bayesian network (MGb).} This ablation adapts PrivBayes~\cite{zhang2017privbayes}: pairs of parents $(u_1, u_2)$ are chosen greedily from the MI of Release~2 while keeping the graph acyclic. Each record still touches $d$ tables, so the record sensitivity is unchanged, while each table grows to accommodate two parents.

\subsubsection*{Transport through SecAgg$^+$}
For MGs and MGr, the tables are sent through Flower SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020} over the integer ring $\mathbb{Z}_M$ with $M = 2^{32}$ (uint32):
\begin{itemize}
    \item Client updates are scaled with \texttt{max\_weight} (unit factor 1), and the quantisation range is set to twice the clipping range, so integers are transmitted exactly, without floating-point rounding.
    \item Under record-level DP, \texttt{clipping\_range} is $100{,}000$, which exceeds the largest possible cell count ($N = 88{,}500$). Under group DP, it is set to $C_{\text{clip}} = N + 6\sqrt{\mu_{\text{sk}}^{\text{group}}/K}$, which covers both the data mass and $6\sigma$ of the discrete noise, so the probability that a noisy count exceeds $C_{\text{clip}}$ is below $10^{-9}$ by the Skellam tail bound.
    \item The noisy counts $c_{k,j} = x_{k,j} + \xi_{k,j} \pmod{2^{32}}$ are sent as uint32 vectors and recovered at the server by a centred lift: $\mathrm{lift}(S) = S$ if $S < 2^{31}$, otherwise $S - 2^{32}$. The cell totals satisfy $\sum_k x_{k,j} \le N = 88{,}500$. Under record-level DP the aggregate noise has $\sigma_{\text{agg}} = \sqrt{\mu_{\text{sk}}} \le 400$. Under group DP with cap $m = 100$, the variance grows quadratically to $\mu_{\text{sk}}^{\text{group}} \le 1.6 \times 10^{9}$, so $\sigma_{\text{agg}} \le 4 \times 10^{4}$ (and $\sigma_k = \sqrt{\mu_{\text{sk}}/K} \le 1.8 \times 10^{4}$ per client share). The noisy total therefore satisfies $|S| \le N + 6\sigma_{\text{agg}} \le 88{,}500 + 2.4 \times 10^{5} \approx 3.3 \times 10^{5} \ll 2^{31} \approx 2.15 \times 10^{9}$; the largest sum lies tens of thousands of standard deviations below the overflow threshold, and the probability of a modular wrap-around is below $10^{-50}$.
    \item The Gaussian variant MGd is simulated in one process over $\mathbb{R}$, and the local-DP variant MGl sends its noisy tables directly without SecAgg$^+$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Privacy Accounting}\label{subsec:privacy_accounting}

\subsubsection*{Analytical zCDP Accounting for the Gaussian Comparator (MGd)}
To provide a continuous comparator for the discrete mechanism, MGd is accounted with analytical zero-concentrated differential privacy (zCDP)~\cite[Proposition~1.3]{bun2016}. For each release $r \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ with noise standard deviation $\sigma_r$ and $\ell_2$ sensitivity $\Delta_{2,r}$, the accumulated parameter $\rho$ and the resulting $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ guarantee are~\cite{bun2016}
\begin{equation}
\rho = \sum_{r=1}^{3} \frac{\Delta_{2,r}^2}{2\sigma_r^2}, \qquad \varepsilon = \rho + 2\sqrt{\rho \ln(1/\delta)}.
\label{eq:zcdp}
\end{equation}
$\rho$ is divided between the three releases by the split $\mathbf{s} = (s_1, s_2, s_3)$, by default $(0.3, 0.1, 0.6)$. The analytical conversion from zCDP to $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ is looser than the numerical conversion from RDP~\cite{balle2020}. At the same nominal $\varepsilon$, part of the utility difference between MGd and MGs therefore comes from the tightness of the conversion and not from the noise mechanism itself.

\subsubsection*{Skellam Mechanism and Accounting (MGs, MGr)}
The nominal guarantees are $\varepsilon \in \{1, 5, 10\}$ with $\delta = 10^{-5}$. The Skellam distribution $\mathrm{Sk}(\mu_{\text{sk}}) = \mathrm{Pois}(\mu_{\text{sk}}/2) - \mathrm{Pois}(\mu_{\text{sk}}/2)$ is the difference of two independent Poisson variables with parameter $\mu_{\text{sk}}/2$; it has zero mean and variance $\mu_{\text{sk}}$~\cite{skellam1946, agarwal2021}. Because it is infinitely divisible, the integer noise shares drawn by the gateways add up exactly under addition modulo $2^{32}$~\cite{agarwal2021}.

The accounting uses the Rényi differential privacy (RDP) bound of Agarwal et~al.~\cite[Corollary~3.6]{agarwal2021}. For an integer order $\lambda > 1$:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon(\lambda) \le \frac{\lambda \Delta_2^2}{2\mu_{\text{sk}}} + \min\left( \frac{(2\lambda - 1)\Delta_2^2 + 6\Delta_1}{4\mu_{\text{sk}}^2}, \frac{3\Delta_1}{2\mu_{\text{sk}}} \right).
\label{eq:skellam_rdp}
\end{equation}
Over the three releases, RDP composes additively~\cite{mironov2017rdp}: $\varepsilon_{\text{total}}(\lambda) = \sum_{r=1}^{3} \varepsilon_r(\lambda)$. The composed RDP curve is converted to $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ with the numerical RDP accountant of Opacus~\cite{yousefpour2021}. Because Equation~\eqref{eq:skellam_rdp} holds only for integer orders, the accountant grid is restricted to $\lambda \in \{2, 3, \dots, 128\}$. A global calibration factor for $\mu_{\text{sk}}$ is found by binary search so that the composed guarantee matches the target $(\varepsilon, \delta)$.

\subsubsection*{Trust Models and Division of the Noise}
The noise is calibrated under three trust assumptions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Distributed DP ($t = K$, main setting):} Each gateway $k$ adds $1/K$ of the total noise variance, $\mu_{\text{sk},k} = \mu_{\text{sk}}/K$. The nominal guarantee holds when all $K$ gateways are honest. When only $h < K$ remain honest, the effective $\varepsilon(h)$ is recomputed with the Opacus RDP accountant from the reduced aggregate variance $\frac{h}{K}\mu_{\text{sk}}$, for $h \in \{1, \dots, K\}$.
    \item \textbf{Threshold DP ($t \le K$):} Each gateway adds $\mu_{\text{sk},k} = \mu_{\text{sk}}/t$, so the target guarantee holds whenever at least $t$ gateways are honest. In SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020}, the masks are shared by Shamir secret sharing with reconstruction threshold $T_{\text{recon}}$. To stop colluding gateways from reconstructing the masking seeds of honest gateways and exposing their individual updates to the server, the number of colluders $c = K - t$ must satisfy $c < T_{\text{recon}}$. The evaluated settings are:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $t = 5$: distributed DP ($c = 0$, $T_{\text{recon}} = 3$), assuming full honest participation;
        \item $t = 3$: threshold DP ($c \le 2 < T_{\text{recon}} = 3$), tolerating up to two dropouts or two semi-honest colluders while the server still sees only the aggregate;
        \item $t = 2$ (hardened threshold DP): to tolerate $c = 3$ colluders, $T_{\text{recon}}$ must be raised to at least 4 (leaving one dropout of tolerance). With $T_{\text{recon}} = 3$, three colluders would hold three shares, enough to reconstruct the honest gateways' masks and reduce the distributed guarantee to local DP. Evaluating $t = 2$ therefore requires $T_{\text{recon}} = 4$.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Local DP (MGl):} Each gateway adds the full noise variance, $\mu_{\text{sk},k} = \mu_{\text{sk}}$. No other gateway and not the server need to be trusted, and SecAgg$^+$ is not used, but the aggregate carries $K$ times the noise variance ($K\mu_{\text{sk}}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Group Privacy and Contribution Bounding}
To protect against the tracing of a session or the re-identification of a capture, group differential privacy is enforced. The sensitivities are scaled by the cap $m$ on the records of one unit~\cite{dwork2014}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_1^{\text{group}} = m \cdot \Delta_1, \qquad \Delta_2^{\text{group}} = m \cdot \Delta_2.
\label{eq:group_sens}
\end{equation}
With Gaussian noise the standard deviation scales linearly, $\sigma^{\text{group}} = m\sigma$; with Skellam noise the parameter scales quadratically, $\mu_{\text{sk}}^{\text{group}} \approx m^2 \mu_{\text{sk}}$.

Group DP holds when (1) each protected unit (TCP stream or capture group) is held entirely by one gateway, through the unit-level partition, and (2) each gateway keeps at most $m$ records of each unit. Under the default record-level DP ($m = 1$), a unit of $\ell$ records receives only an inflated group bound. With approximate $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ composition, the group relaxation $\ell e^{(\ell-1)\varepsilon}\delta$ becomes vacuous for $\ell \ge 3$ at $\varepsilon = 10$, so the group guarantee is computed by zCDP scaling for Gaussian mechanisms~\cite{bun2016},
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\text{group}} = \ell^2 \rho,
\label{eq:group_zcdp}
\end{equation}
converted to $(\varepsilon_{\text{group}}, \delta)$ with Equation~\eqref{eq:zcdp}, or by recomputing Equation~\eqref{eq:skellam_rdp} with $\Delta \to \ell\Delta$ for Skellam mechanisms. The caps $m \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ for streams and $m \in \{25, 50, 100\}$ for capture groups are target privacy policies, not tuned model parameters.

\subsubsection*{Validity Constraints and Verification}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Linear aggregation:} Distributed DP under secure addition is valid only when each release is a single additive sum. It cannot be applied to the multi-epoch DP-SGD of the CVAE.
    \item \textbf{Uniform protection:} The noise is never reduced selectively for the rare classes, as that would weaken the protection of minority-class records below the announced $\varepsilon$.
    \item \textbf{Values sent in clear:} Flower sends the client sizes $n_i$ (\texttt{num\_examples}) in clear, outside the DP perimeter.
    \item \textbf{Independent verification:} Every reported $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ is recomputed independently by \texttt{eval/dp\_check.py}. An audit flag (F5) is raised when the recomputed value differs from the reported one by more than 10\% ($|\varepsilon_{\text{recomp}} - \varepsilon|/\varepsilon > 0.10$).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{DP-SGD Accounting for the Federated CVAE}
The deep baseline is trained with differentially private stochastic gradient descent (DP-SGD)~\cite{abadi2016dpsgd} adapted to federated learning. At each local step, each client clips the per-example gradients to norm $C$ and adds Gaussian noise to the mini-batch sum (Equation~\eqref{eq:dpsgd} in Section~\ref{subsec:cvae}), using Opacus 1.6.0~\cite{yousefpour2021}. Mini-batches are formed by Poisson sampling with rate $q_k = B_k/n_k$, where $B_k = \min(B_{\text{batch}}, n_k)$ keeps $q_k \le 1$. The local privacy loss $\varepsilon_k$ is accumulated over $R$ rounds and $E$ local epochs with the RDP accountant of the sampled Gaussian mechanism~\cite{mironov2019}.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Per-client calibration and amplification by subsampling:} The noise multiplier $\sigma_k$ is calibrated per client from its size $n_k$ and its number of steps. Under a skewed partition, clients with very few rows ($n_k \approx 500 \le B_{\text{batch}}$) have $q_k = 1$ and lose the amplification by subsampling, so their $\sigma_k$ must be much larger to reach the target. Because steps are discrete and the calibration grid is finite, the realised $\varepsilon_k$ differ slightly between clients. The federation is reported conservatively as the worst client~\cite{mironov2019},
    \begin{equation}
    \varepsilon_{\text{effective}} = \max_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \varepsilon_k,
    \label{eq:eps_effective}
    \end{equation}
    together with the median over clients; $\delta < 1/n_k$ holds for every client.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetry between the routes:} In M3, SecAgg$^+$~\cite{bell2020} hides the updates from the server in transit, but the noise is still added locally by each client without sharing across clients. The CVAE therefore works under local DP-SGD, whereas FedDP-Marginal benefits from distributed DP under secure aggregation.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Formal Privacy Perimeter}
The formal DP perimeter is as follows (see also Section~\ref{subsec:threat_model}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Protected by $\varepsilon$:} All 37 attributes (and their 64-bin discrete states) of the clients' training records. The coarse cut-points are derived from the noisy histograms of Release~1 and are therefore covered by post-processing~\cite{dwork2014}.
    \item \textbf{Public side information outside $\varepsilon$:} Class labels $y$ and the global class quotas; client sizes $n_i$ (\texttt{num\_examples} sent in clear by Flower); and the global preprocessing parameters (means, standard deviations, $\log(1+x)$ choices, and $\pm 5\sigma$ clipping bounds) computed centrally on the training split. As stated in Section~\ref{sec:methodology_limitations}, treating normalisation parameters as public is a common convention in tabular benchmarks but lies outside the formal accounting.
    \item \textbf{Leakage through selection:} Hyper-parameters are selected on the real validation split without a private selection mechanism (Section~\ref{subsec:hpo}). The reported $\varepsilon$ is the nominal guarantee of training, not an end-to-end guarantee that includes hyper-parameter selection.
    \item \textbf{Relaxation parameter:} $\delta = 10^{-5}$ in all evaluations ($1/n \approx 1.13 \times 10^{-5}$ for $n = 88{,}500$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Federated CVAE, DP-SGD and SecAgg$^+$ (Baseline)}\label{subsec:cvae}

\subsubsection*{CVAE Architecture and Loss}
The deep baseline is a label-conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE)~\cite{sohn2015cvae}. The encoder $q_\phi(z \mid x, y)$ maps the features $x \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$ ($D = 60$) and the one-hot label $y \in \{0, 1\}^{C}$ to latent parameters $(\mu_z, \log \sigma_z^2) \in \mathbb{R}^{2 d_z}$, and the decoder $p_\theta(x \mid z, y)$ reconstructs the input.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{No batch normalisation:} The CVAE has no batch-normalisation layers. This is required for per-example gradients to be independent, so that Opacus can clip each example's gradient without coupling between examples~\cite{yousefpour2021}. The parameters form a fixed-size vector, compatible with FedAvg and SecAgg$^+$.
    \item \textbf{Loss:} The model minimises the $\beta$-weighted conditional variational loss~\cite{sohn2015cvae, higgins2017}:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{CVAE}}(\phi, \theta) ={}& \sum_{b \in \text{num}} \|x_b - \hat{x}_b\|_2^2 + \sum_{b \in \text{bin}} \mathrm{BCE}(x_b, \hat{x}_b) + \sum_{g \in \text{cat}} \mathrm{CE}(x_g, \hat{x}_g) \\
    &+ \beta \cdot D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q_\phi(z \mid x, y) \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})\big),
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:cvae_loss}
    \end{equation}
    where num, bin, and cat denote the numeric, binary, and categorical feature blocks.
    \item \textbf{Warm-up and early stopping:} $\beta$ rises linearly from 0 to its target 0.5 over the first 10 local epochs (the first 5 global rounds with $E = 2$). Training is monitored by early stopping on the validation loss.
    \item \textbf{Default parameters:} latent dimension $d_z = 16$, hidden layers $[128, 64]$, learning rate $\eta = 10^{-3}$, batch size $B_{\text{batch}} = 256$, and $\beta = 0.5$.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Federated Optimisation}
Training uses Federated Averaging (FedAvg)~\cite{mcmahan2017} over $R = 30$ global rounds with $E = 2$ local epochs per round:
\begin{equation}
w^{r+1} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{n_k}{\sum_{j} n_j} w_k^{r+1}.
\label{eq:fedavg}
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Optimiser state:} The Adam states are re-initialised at the start of each global round.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic order:} Client updates are aggregated in a fixed order of client identifiers, so that runs are exactly reproducible.
    \item \textbf{Execution:} The federation is simulated with Flower 1.39.0~\cite{beutel2020} and Ray 2.59.0 actors that share the CPU of one machine.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{DP-SGD at the Clients}
When DP is used (M1 and M3), clients run DP-SGD with Opacus 1.6.0~\cite{yousefpour2021}. At each local step, the per-example gradients are clipped to norm $C$ and Gaussian noise is added before the update~\cite{abadi2016dpsgd}:
\begin{equation}
\bar{g}_i = \frac{g_i}{\max(1, \|g_i\|_2 / C)}, \qquad \tilde{g} = \frac{1}{B_k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{B_k} \bar{g}_i + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_k^2 C^2 \mathbf{I})\right),
\label{eq:dpsgd}
\end{equation}
where $B_k = \min(B_{\text{batch}}, n_k)$ gives a valid normalising constant for small clients.

\subsubsection*{SecAgg$^+$ Configuration and Bounds on the Weights}
For M2 and M3, the updates are aggregated with Flower's \texttt{SecAggPlusWorkflow} and \texttt{secaggplus\_mod}~\cite{bell2020}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Protocol topology:} \texttt{num\_shares} $= 5$ and \texttt{reconstruction\_threshold} $= 3$, which tolerates up to two dropped clients per round.
    \item \textbf{Bounds:} \texttt{clipping\_range} $= 16$ (checked against the largest absolute parameter value $|w|$), uniform quantisation over $Q = 2^{22}$ levels, \texttt{max\_weight} $= 100{,}000$ (above the largest client size, $n_k \le N = 88{,}500$), and modulus $M = 2^{32}$ (uint32).
    \item \textbf{Weight scaling and modular bound:} To compute the exact weighted average of Equation~\eqref{eq:fedavg} without overflow in $\mathbb{Z}_{2^{32}}$, each client scales its update by its relative weight before quantisation. Let $\mathcal{K}_{\text{active}}$ be the clients active in the round, whose sizes $n_k$ are sent in clear (Section~\ref{subsec:privacy_accounting}). Each client computes $p_k = n_k / \sum_{j \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{active}}} n_j \in (0, 1]$ and scales its clipped update $\bar{\mathbf{w}}_k$ ($\|\bar{\mathbf{w}}_k\|_\infty \le 16$) to $\mathbf{u}_k = p_k \bar{\mathbf{w}}_k$, which still satisfies $\|\mathbf{u}_k\|_\infty \le 16$ because $p_k \le 1$. It then quantises $\mathbf{u}_k$ uniformly into integers $\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_k \in \{0, 1, \dots, Q - 1\}$. Through SecAgg$^+$, the server receives the modular sum~\cite{bell2020}
    \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{S} = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{active}}} \tilde{\mathbf{u}}_k \pmod{2^{32}}.
    \label{eq:secagg_sum}
    \end{equation}
    Since each entry is at most $Q - 1$, the unmasked sum over $K = 5$ clients satisfies
    \begin{equation}
    \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{active}}} \tilde{u}_k \le K (Q - 1) = 5 \times (2^{22} - 1) \approx 2.097 \times 10^{7} \ll 2^{31} < 2^{32}.
    \label{eq:secagg_bound}
    \end{equation}
    The server applies the centred lift and the affine dequantisation to recover $\sum_{k} p_k \bar{\mathbf{w}}_k$ directly. Because $\sum_k p_k = 1$, the server does not divide by $\sum_k n_k$. This gives the exact weighted FedAvg while avoiding 32-bit wrap-around, which would occur if the updates were scaled by the raw counts $n_k$: $\sum_k n_k (Q - 1) \approx 88{,}500 \times 2^{22} \approx 3.71 \times 10^{11} > 2^{32}$.
    \item \textbf{Protocol tests:} T-SA1 (deviation from plain FedAvg $\le 10^{-4}$), T-SA2 (masked vectors uncorrelated with the true weights and uniformly distributed), and T-SA3 (robustness to simulated client dropouts). SecAgg$^+$ provides transport privacy only and receives no credit in the DP accounting.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Generation of Synthetic Samples}
After training, the encoder $q_\phi$ is discarded. Synthetic samples are generated by drawing $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ and decoding with $p_\theta(x \mid z, y = c)$~\cite{sohn2015cvae}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sampling by block:} Bernoulli sampling for binary features, softmax sampling for categorical blocks, and inverse standardisation for numeric features.
    \item \textbf{Plain decoder:} Uses the final decoder outputs directly. It stays within the formal DP guarantee and is the format used in all DP comparisons.
    \item \textbf{Residual-noise decoder:} Restores class-conditional feature variances estimated on the centralised training data (outside $\varepsilon$). It is used only as a non-private reference.
    \item \textbf{Options of round O1:} DP-computed residual statistics (budget fraction \texttt{stat\_frac}$\,\times\,\varepsilon$) and client class weighting (\texttt{cw\_power}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hyper-parameter Selection and Search Spaces}\label{subsec:hpo}

\subsubsection*{Optimisation Protocol and Integrity of Selection}
Hyper-parameters are optimised with Optuna~\cite{akiba2019} using the Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE)~\cite{bergstra2011}. All searches use the validation split only, so the test split is never touched. The objective is the utility for the downstream IDS: the validation macro-F1 of a Random Forest~\cite{breiman2001} (100 trees during tuning to save time, 200 trees in the final protocols) trained on synthetic records (5,000 rows per class) and evaluated on the real validation traffic (TSTR). The test split stays locked and is evaluated once, on the final configurations.

Selecting hyper-parameters on the real validation split leaks information about the training data through the trial models. Private selection algorithms~\cite{mcsherry2007, liu2019, papernot2022} bound this extra privacy cost; this study reports the nominal $\varepsilon$ of training and documents the number of trials of each search so that the selection can be accounted for.

\subsubsection*{Separate Search Spaces}
Tuning is split into five search spaces (Table~\ref{tab:search_spaces}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Space (a), centralised CVAE without DP (phase 7):} 30 TPE trials on centralised data over 30 epochs, exploring $d_z \in \{8, 16, 32\}$, hidden width $\in \{64, 128, 256\}$, $\beta \in [0.1, 2.0]$ (log-uniform), and $\eta \in [10^{-4}, 3 \times 10^{-3}]$ (log-uniform). Output: \texttt{configs/best\_cvae.yaml}.
    \item \textbf{Space (b), CVAE with DP at proxy scale (\texttt{tune-dp}):} To limit the cost, initial DP-SGD tuning runs 30 trials on a single proxy client with 20\% of the data at $\varepsilon = 5$ (one seed per trial). The best candidates are verified in true federated training (\texttt{verify-dp}), selected by validation macro-F1, with ties within 0.01 broken by the lowest validation loss. It explores $d_z \in \{8, 16, 32\}$, hidden width $\in \{32, 64, 128, 256\}$, $B_{\text{batch}} \in \{128, 256, 512, 1024\}$, $\beta \in [0.1, 2.0]$, $\eta \in [2 \times 10^{-4}, 5 \times 10^{-3}]$ (log-uniform), and clipping norm $C \in [0.1, 20.0]$ (log-uniform). Output: \texttt{configs/best\_cvae\_dp.yaml} (family M1d-t21).
    \item \textbf{Space (c), CVAE with DP at full federated scale (\texttt{tune-dp-full}, round O1):} Full federated optimisation over 5 clients with seed 0, with 30 trials for $\varepsilon = 5$, 28 for $\varepsilon = 1$, and 12 for $\varepsilon = 10$. It explores $d_z \in \{8, 16, 32\}$, hidden width $\in \{64, 128\}$ (256 removed for memory reasons), $B_{\text{batch}} \in \{256, 512, 1024, 2048\}$, $\beta \in [0.1, 2.0]$, $\eta \in [5 \times 10^{-4}, 2 \times 10^{-2}]$ (log-uniform), $C \in [0.5, 50.0]$ (log-uniform), rounds $R \in \{15, 30, 50\}$, local epochs $E \in \{1, 2, 4\}$, client class weighting \texttt{cw\_power} $\in \{0, 0.5, 1.0\}$, DP residual budget fraction \texttt{stat\_frac} $\in \{0.02, 0.05, 0.10\}$, and residual clipping \texttt{res\_clip} $\in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0\}$. Output: \texttt{configs/best\_cvae\_dp\_full.yaml} (M1o, M3o).
    \item \textbf{Space (d), FedDP-Marginal:} Discrete grid search scored by the mean over three seeds on the validation split. It explores the coarse bins $B_{\text{coarse}} \in \{4, 8, 16, 32\}$ crossed with the budget splits $\mathbf{s} \in \{(0.3, 0.1, 0.6), (0.5, 0.1, 0.4), (0.15, 0.05, 0.8)\}$; the dependency measure (plug-in MI, Miller--Madow MI $\hat{I}_{\text{MM}}$~\cite{miller1955}, or normalised MI), with a stability check that measures the Jaccard similarity~\cite{jaccard1912} of the tree edges across partition seeds,
    \begin{equation}
    J(T_a^\star, T_b^\star) = \frac{|E(T_a^\star) \cap E(T_b^\star)|}{|E(T_a^\star) \cup E(T_b^\star)|};
    \label{eq:jaccard}
    \end{equation}
    the second-order structure of MGb (parent search, bins specific to rare classes, number of fine bins); and the refinement options (inverse-variance merging, Laplace smoothing $\alpha_{\text{smooth}} \in \{0.1, 1.0, 10.0\}$, root chosen by noisy MI). The policy settings for unit caps ($m_{\text{stream}} \in \{1, 2, 4\}$, $m_{\text{capture}} \in \{25, 50, 100\}$) and honest thresholds ($t \in \{5, 3\}$, and $t = 2$ with $T_{\text{recon}} = 4$) are evaluated in \texttt{best\_group\_dp.yaml}.
    \item \textbf{Space (e), downstream IDS:} Choice between Random Forest (200 trees) and MLP (hidden layers $[128, 64]$, early stopping) for each protocol by validation macro-F1; the rare-class augmentation ratio of TAugR $\in \{0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$ (seed 0); and class-probability weights $w_c \in \{0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0\}$, by a two-round coordinate search averaged over three seeds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{Multi-Seed Screening Rule and Asymmetries}
For the ablations within space (d), candidates are screened with an adaptation of the one-standard-error rule~\cite{hastie2009}:
\begin{equation}
\text{Macro-F1}_{\text{candidate}} > \text{Macro-F1}_{\text{baseline}} + \mathrm{std}_{\text{seeds}}(\text{baseline}),
\label{eq:screening}
\end{equation}
where the means and sample standard deviations ($\text{ddof} = 1$) are computed over the validation seeds $\{0, 1, 2\}$, and the baseline is the main MGs generator at the same $\varepsilon$.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A coarse filter, not a test:} This condition only prunes unpromising configurations during the validation search; it is not a formal hypothesis test. Formal comparisons between configurations use the paired bootstrap decision rule~\cite{efron1993} defined in Section~3.3.7.
    \item \textbf{Unpaired comparison and expected false passes:} The rule compares candidate and baseline means over independent seeds rather than paired differences per seed. Under the null hypothesis of equal performance and equal variance ($\sigma_{\text{cand}} = \sigma_{\text{base}} = \sigma$), the difference of two three-seed means has variance $\mathrm{Var}(\bar{X}_{\text{cand}} - \bar{X}_{\text{base}}) = 2\sigma^2/3$. The offset $\mathrm{std}_{\text{seeds}}(\text{baseline}) \approx \sigma$ thus corresponds to a standard normal score $z = \sigma/(\sqrt{2/3}\,\sigma) = \sqrt{1.5} \approx 1.22$, that is $p = 1 - \Phi(1.22) \approx 0.11$. In a grid of at least 12 candidates, about 11\% of null candidates (about 1.3 candidates) are expected to pass by chance alone. Equation~\eqref{eq:screening} is therefore only a high-throughput sieve; the final statistical decision requires the paired bootstrap rule over shared partition seeds (Section~3.3.7).
    \item \textbf{Asymmetry of the tuning protocols:} Because iterative DP-SGD is expensive, the CVAE searches used one seed per trial (space (c) was tuned on seed 0 and then evaluated on all seeds), whereas FedDP-Marginal was selected on the mean of three seeds on the validation split.
    \item \textbf{Reporting:} The final test evaluations are run on the locked test split with seeds $\{0, 1, 2\}$. Validation and test scores are results and are reported in Chapter~4.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Measures Supporting Reliability and Reproducibility}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Central run ledger:} Each run appends one row to \texttt{results/runs.csv} with a configuration hash and the Git commit of the code that produced it. The environment record of the reference run shows commit \texttt{9ee2a85e} and configuration hash \texttt{8c86c0b143d4}.
    \item \textbf{Checkpoint and resume:} Long training jobs save state; re-running a command compares against the ledger and skips completed configurations.
    \item \textbf{Automated reporting:} The tables, intervals, and figures of the final report are generated by code (\texttt{ppfeddata aggregate}, \texttt{ppfeddata interpret}); no result is typed by hand. A check recomputes macro-F1 from the saved prediction files and compares it with the ledger.
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility bundle:} \texttt{results/repro/} holds the exact package list (\texttt{pip\_freeze.txt}, identical to \texttt{requirements-lock.txt}), the configuration files, the environment record, and a manifest with SHA-256 digests of the ledger, the split and schema manifests, and the report.
    \item \textbf{Acceptance command:} \texttt{python -m ppfeddata.cli accept} checks the repository against the Definition of Done and writes a checklist of twelve items (D1--D10, with D6 split into three). This command does not run the test suite; the suite is run separately.
    \item \textbf{Clean-copy re-execution:} Phases 1--5 (inventory, harmonisation, sampling, preprocessing, leakage check) were re-run from the raw data in a clean copy of the repository with a fresh virtual environment, and the processed train, validation, and test arrays were compared with those of the main checkout. Phases 6--14 were not re-run in the clean copy.
    \item \textbf{Controlled trial:} A trial run of all ten configurations of the base matrix with seed 0 was compared row by row with the ledger of the main checkout (\texttt{SPEC\_DEVIATIONS.md}, 11.6).
    \item \textbf{Leakage checks:} The data pipeline is guarded by five checks (C1--C5, Section~3.2.6), and the real test split is verified against a stored hash.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations of the Methodology}\label{sec:methodology_limitations}
The limitations below arise from the design of the study and the scope of the techniques. The full list is maintained in \texttt{limitations.py}, which feeds the final report and the README; this section groups them. None of the statements below reports a result.

\subsubsection*{Data and Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Only one dataset is used; the study offers no evidence about other traffic or networks.
    \item The Normal traffic was re-exported from the original PCAP files and also contains the packets sent by the broker, which the authors' export settings exclude (Section~\ref{subsec:dataset}).
    \item Records are single packets, and the generators produce packets independently. Session-level temporal structure is therefore not reproduced, and the time-based features depend on how the captures were exported.
    \item Eight of the eleven sub-classes originate from a single capture file, so their validation and test rows are consecutive blocks of the same capture and not other captures. Removing TCP streams that straddle two blocks (\texttt{drop\_cross\_block\_streams}) reduces leakage between splits but biases the sample toward short connections; the share removed per sub-class is documented in the split manifest.
    \item The pipeline keeps only rows with protocol TCP or MQTT, keeps the first element of multi-valued cells, clips the tail of the time-gap feature at $5\sigma$, and treats nearly empty columns with the statistics of the rows where they apply plus an indicator.
    \item DoS and DDoS are merged in the six-class mode used for the experiment matrix.
    \item The validation and test splits are class-balanced (5,000 test rows per class) and do not follow the class proportions of real traffic.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Privacy Scope}
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\varepsilon$ is stated at record level (one packet) by default. Stream- and capture-level protection is optional for FedDP-Marginal and requires each unit to be held by one client and capped at $m$ rows. Under record-level $\varepsilon$, an attack session or capture is protected only through the weaker group bound~\cite{dwork2014}.
    \item Class labels, class counts, and client sizes are not protected (Section~\ref{subsec:threat_model}).
    \item Normalisation statistics are computed centrally on the pooled training split, as if each client had shared its data once. A real federation would have to compute them securely or fix them beforehand.
    \item Hyper-parameter selection on the real validation split is not private and lies outside $\varepsilon$.
    \item Distributed DP assumes that enough clients add their noise share; the guarantee degrades as the number of honest clients falls, which is why $\varepsilon$ is reported as a function of $h$.
    \item Accounting differs between variants: the Skellam variant uses the RDP bound of Agarwal et~al.~\cite{agarwal2021} and the RDP-to-$(\varepsilon, \delta)$ conversion of Opacus~\cite{yousefpour2021}, while the Gaussian variant uses zCDP~\cite{bun2016} with a looser conversion, so part of the difference between the two at the same $\varepsilon$ comes from the conversion and not from the mechanism.
    \item Malicious gateways (poisoning, Byzantine behaviour) are outside the threat model.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Generators}
\begin{itemize}
    \item FedDP-Marginal represents dependencies with a Chow--Liu tree~\cite{chow1968}, so each attribute depends on one parent; a second-order structure (MGb)~\cite{zhang2017privbayes} is only an option.
    \item Only one CVAE architecture is studied, and its hyper-parameter search used one seed per trial.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Evaluation and Statistics}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Three seeds are used for every configuration. Federated training can be sensitive to very small numerical perturbations, so a standard deviation over three seeds is a rough estimate.
    \item The bootstrap intervals and decision rules reflect the sampling of test rows (or whole streams) and of training seeds; they do not cover the choice of capture groups, the hyper-parameters, or the data sampling. Sensitivity A4 explores only two alternative choices of validation and test groups.
    \item The decision thresholds (for example the membership-inference AUC bound of 0.55, the $\varepsilon$ bound of 5, and the overhead ratio of 3) are heuristics fixed by the research team and not derived from data.
    \item Each membership-inference family tests one attack at record level and does not test group-level inference; each attack must pass a positive control to be informative.
    \item The decision rule does not use $p$-values or a multiple-comparison correction.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Execution Platform}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Federated learning is simulated on one CPU machine with Ray actors; no network latency, packet loss, or constrained-device behaviour is measured, and the reported times per round are compute times on this machine.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Baselines Not Compared and Parts Not Run}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Not compared:} FLAIM and AIM (the Private-PGM dependency was not installed), DP-CTGAN or other deep generators in the federation, federated SMOTE, and a directly trained federated classifier with distributed DP.
    \item \textbf{Written but not run after the change of primary generator:} M3f (distributed DP-FedSGD for the CVAE) and the SecAgg$^+$ quantisation-width sweep for the CVAE. Robustness branches A2 and A3 have configuration files but no runner.
\end{itemize}
