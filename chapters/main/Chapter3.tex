\chapter{Methodology}
Describes the research design, data collection methods, and analysis techniques.

\section{Research Design}

\subsection{Research Type and Design Overview}
This study is a quantitative, controlled experimental study carried out on one published network-traffic dataset, DoS/DDoS-MQTT-IoT~\cite{alatram2023}. In the terminology of the FPT thesis guide, it combines Experiments with Archival Research: the independent variables (generator, privacy mechanism, privacy budget) are manipulated by the researchers, while the traffic itself is existing recorded data rather than data collected specifically for this study.

\subsubsection*{Research Object}
The object of study is a framework, PP-FedData, that lets several IoT/MQTT gateways jointly produce synthetic network-traffic records without any gateway releasing its raw packets, so that an intrusion detection system (IDS) can be trained on data that no single party could pool. The framework is evaluated along four dimensions: the utility of the synthetic data for the IDS, the privacy guarantee attached to the released artefacts, the communication and computation overhead, and the fit of each configuration to a stated deployment requirement.

\subsubsection*{Experimental Control}
Internal validity rests on holding everything except the studied factors fixed. Every configuration in the experiment matrix (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_configs}) is run with:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the same train/validation/test split, formed by capture group, with a real test split that is locked and never used for tuning (train 88,500, validation 12,000, test 30,000 rows; class quotas fixed in the configuration);
    \item the same non-IID partition of the training rows over $K = 5$ clients, drawn from a Dirichlet distribution with concentration $\alpha = 0.5$~\cite{hsu2019} and fixed by the seed;
    \item the same preprocessing ($\log(1+x)$ transformation of skewed columns, standardisation, clipping at $\pm 5\sigma$, one-hot encoding of categorical columns), which turns the harmonised packet attributes into 60 model input columns;
    \item the same downstream classifiers: a Random Forest~\cite{breiman2001} with 200 trees and a multilayer perceptron with hidden layers $[128, 64]$;
    \item the same three random seeds $\{0, 1, 2\}$;
    \item the same real test split for every reported score.
\end{itemize}

Under this design, differences between configurations are attributable to the generator and to the protection mechanism, subject to the limits listed in Section~\ref{sec:methodology_limitations}. The design does not claim that extraneous influences are eliminated: hyper-parameter selection, the choice of capture groups, and the number of seeds all remain sources of variation, and Section~\ref{sec:methodology_limitations} states how far each is covered.

\subsubsection*{Evolution of the Design}
The study was originally designed around a federated conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE)~\cite{sohn2015}, which is the premise of the project. After a first evaluation round, the framework adopted a marginal-based generator, FedDP-Marginal, as its primary generator and retained the CVAE as a baseline. The decision is recorded with its date and rationale in the project's deviation log (\texttt{docs/SPEC\_DEVIATIONS.md}, entries O1.9 and O2.3); Chapter~4 presents the evidence and Chapter~5 discusses it. The CVAE configurations remain in the matrix so that the comparison in RQ2 is made under identical federation, privacy, and secure-aggregation conditions.

\subsection{System Architecture and the Two Generator Routes}

\subsubsection*{Roles}
The system involves three primary roles (Figure~\ref{fig:system_arch}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Gateways (federated learning clients):} The main study uses $K = 5$ gateways. Each gateway holds the traffic it observed and is trusted with its own data. Because the partition is non-IID, some gateways have few or no examples of some attack classes.
    \item \textbf{Aggregation server:} The server coordinates the rounds, combines client contributions and, after training or table release, produces the synthetic data. It is modelled as honest-but-curious (Section~\ref{subsec:threat_model}).
    \item \textbf{Downstream IDS:} A Random Forest and a multilayer perceptron are trained on synthetic data (TSTR) or on real plus synthetic data (TAug, TAugR, local augmentation) and are evaluated on the real test split.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Where Synthetic Data is Generated}
Synthetic records are generated at the server, from the global model (CVAE decoder) or from the released global tables (marginal counts and dependency tree), by sampling conditionally on the class label. They are not generated at the gateways. The sampling functions are implemented in \texttt{models/generate.py} (CVAE) and \texttt{models/marginal.py} (FedDP-Marginal).

\subsubsection*{What the Server Sees}
The visibility of client contributions depends on the configuration and must not be stated in general terms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item M2, M3, MGs, and MGr use Flower's SecAgg$^+$ protocol~\cite{bell2020, beutel2020}: the server receives masked vectors and can recover only their sum.
    \item B3 and M1 use plain FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017}: the server sees each client's model update; in M1 the update already carries noise added by DP-SGD.
    \item MGl (local DP): each client adds the full noise to its own tables and sends them without secure aggregation, so the server sees each client's noisy tables.
    \item MGd and MGb simulate the distributed sum inside one process; they do not pass through the SecAgg$^+$ protocol.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{The Two Generator Routes}
Table~\ref{tab:two_routes} contrasts the two routes. FedDP-Marginal exchanges counts of discrete attribute combinations in three releases; the CVAE exchanges continuous model weights over many FedAvg rounds. The difference matters for privacy accounting. Each marginal release is a single additive aggregate with bounded sensitivity, so the calibrated noise can be divided among the clients and the sum computed through secure aggregation. DP-SGD~\cite{abadi2016}, by contrast, performs many local non-linear steps per round, and noise added by one client passes through the other clients' training before aggregation; the project therefore does not claim distributed DP for the CVAE route (\texttt{SPEC\_DEVIATIONS.md}, O2.1).

\subsection{Threat Model, Trust Assumptions and Privacy Units}\label{subsec:threat_model}

\subsubsection*{Adversary and Trust Assumptions}
The aggregation server is assumed to be honest-but-curious, also called semi-honest~\cite{goldreich2004}: it follows the protocol but may analyse every message it receives. Gateways hold their own traffic and are trusted with it. The study considers four trust settings, which differ in how much the privacy guarantee depends on other gateways:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Base setting:} All $K$ gateways follow the protocol and each adds its designated share of the noise ($1/K$ of the variance in the distributed variants).
    \item \textbf{$h$ honest gateways:} The guarantee is also reported when only $h \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ gateways add their share; the remaining gateways may collude with the server or drop out. The reported $\varepsilon$ then grows as $h$ decreases. For the distributed FedDP-Marginal variant, the project reports $\varepsilon$ for every $h$; Chapter~4 gives the values.
    \item \textbf{Threshold $t$:} The per-client noise is calibrated so that the target $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ holds whenever at least $t$ gateways contribute their share. Each client then adds $1/t$ of the calibrated variance. The default is $t = K$.
    \item \textbf{Local DP (MGl):} Each gateway adds the full noise itself, so no other gateway needs to be trusted~\cite{kasiviswanathan2011}; the total noise variance in the aggregate grows with the number of clients.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Out of Scope}
Active attacks by malicious gateways, namely data poisoning, model poisoning, and Byzantine behaviour, are outside the threat model. Statements in this thesis about privacy do not extend to them.

\subsubsection*{Roles of Secure Aggregation and Differential Privacy}
The two mechanisms address different threats and are not interchangeable (Table~\ref{tab:protection_mechanisms}).

\subsubsection*{Privacy Unit}
The default unit is the record, that is, one network packet. An $\varepsilon$ at record level protects the presence of one packet. For FedDP-Marginal, two larger units are available: the TCP stream (suffix \texttt{-strm\{m\}}) and the capture (suffix \texttt{-cap\{m\}}). Their guarantee holds only under two conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item each unit is held entirely by one client (the partition is made by unit, not by row);
    \item each client uses at most $m$ rows of each unit.
\end{enumerate}
When both hold, a release of $T$ tables has sensitivities $\Delta_1 = m \cdot T$ in $\ell_1$ and $\Delta_2 = m \sqrt{T}$ in $\ell_2$ (\texttt{group\_dp.py}), and the noise is calibrated to these values. The CVAE routes keep record-level $\varepsilon$.

\subsubsection*{Why the Unit Must Be Stated}
Packets of one stream, and packets of one capture, are correlated. Under record-level $\varepsilon$, a set of $k$ correlated records from the same source is protected only through group privacy, which weakens the guarantee to roughly $k\varepsilon$ with a correspondingly larger $\delta$~\cite{dwork2014}. A statement of $\varepsilon$ without its unit therefore says little about whether an attack session as a whole is concealed. The unit statistics of the data are: the training split contains 76,463 TCP streams and 147 capture groups, and a capture contains between 62 and 1,277 rows.

\subsubsection*{Public Side Information (Not Covered by $\varepsilon$)}
The following are treated as known to every party and are outside the accounting:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the class labels and the number of examples per class;
    \item client sizes $n_i$, where they are transmitted in clear (Flower's \texttt{num\_examples} in the CVAE routes);
    \item the normalisation statistics, the $\log(1+x)$ choices, the category lists, and the mask of unused categories, computed centrally on the pooled training split before partitioning;
    \item the hyper-parameter selection, performed on the real validation split.
\end{itemize}
In the FedDP-Marginal route the client reports \texttt{num\_examples} as a fixed public constant, so client sizes do not travel through that field (\texttt{SPEC\_DEVIATIONS.md}, O2.3).

\subsubsection*{Standard Formulation for the Thesis}
We assume an honest-but-curious aggregation server that follows the protocol but may analyse every message it receives. Gateways hold their own traffic and are trusted with it. In the base setting every gateway follows the protocol and adds its share of the noise; we also report the guarantee when only $h$ of the $K$ gateways do so, and an optional threshold $t$ calibrates the noise so that the target $\varepsilon$ holds whenever at least $t$ gateways add their share (the others may collude with the server or drop out). In the local-DP variant each gateway adds the full noise, so no other gateway needs to be trusted. Malicious (poisoning or Byzantine) gateways are out of scope.

Secure aggregation hides each gateway's contribution from the server, which learns only the sum; it does not limit what the aggregate reveals and receives no privacy credit. Differential privacy bounds what the released tables, model, or synthetic data reveal about one record (one packet) with $\delta = 10^{-5}$. Class labels, class counts, and client sizes are treated as public side information and are not protected.

\subsection{Requirement-Driven Recommendation Rule}\label{subsec:req_rule}

\subsubsection*{Motivation and Relation to Rule R6}
The original specification fixed a single rule, R6: among the configurations whose membership-inference AUC is at most 0.55, whose $\varepsilon$ (maximum over clients and worst seed) is at most 5 when DP is used, and whose time per round is at most three times that of B3, choose the one with the highest TAug macro-F1 of the Random Forest. Ties within the seed noise are broken by the strength of the formal protection. The thresholds are heuristics fixed by the research team (Section~\ref{sec:methodology_limitations}). R6 is retained as a reference rule. A single fixed criterion cannot express deployments that differ in trust, bandwidth, and privacy unit, so the framework adds a requirement-driven rule, implemented as \texttt{ppfeddata recommend} (\texttt{recommend.py}), which takes a deployment requirement as input.

\subsubsection*{Requirement Formulation}
A requirement is the tuple:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{R} = \langle & \text{Trust}_{\text{server}}, \text{Trust}_{\text{clients}}, \varepsilon_{\max}, \text{BW}_{\text{round}}, \text{BW}_{\text{total}}, \\
& \text{Rounds}_{\max}, \text{Unit}, \text{Priority}, \text{Recall}_{\text{rare},\min}, \text{MIA}_{\max}, h \rangle
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\text{Trust}_{\text{server}}$ and $\text{Trust}_{\text{clients}}$ state whether the server and the other clients are trusted; $\varepsilon_{\max}$ is the record-level (or unit-level) budget, or none if no DP is required; $\text{BW}_{\text{round}}$, $\text{BW}_{\text{total}}$, and $\text{Rounds}_{\max}$ bound the gateway bandwidth per round, over the whole run, and in rounds; $\text{Unit} \in \{\text{packet}, \text{stream}, \text{capture}\}$ is the unit $\varepsilon$ must cover; $\text{Priority} \in \{\text{macro}, \text{binary}, \text{rare}\}$ selects TSTR macro-F1, binary F1, or mean rare-class recall; $\text{Recall}_{\text{rare},\min}$ is a floor on the rare-class recall; $\text{MIA}_{\max}$ is the maximum acceptable membership-inference AUC; and $h$ is the number of clients assumed to add their noise share.

\subsubsection*{Procedure}
Inputs are the rows of the scorecard, which are produced from the run ledger; no figure is typed by hand. The rule applies five steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Validity filter:} A row is considered only if its reported quantities correspond to what its name claims. For example, a DP configuration with a component computed from data outside the $\varepsilon$ accounting (residual noise computed from real training data) is not valid; a directly federated classifier is a reference and is excluded from the generator front.
    \item \textbf{Trust filter:} If the server is not trusted, only rows with secure aggregation or local DP remain. If clients may collude, the $\varepsilon$ used for a distributed-DP row is the one that holds with the assumed number of honest clients (one honest client by default, or $h$ when it is specified); local-DP rows keep their $\varepsilon$ because each client adds the full noise.
    \item \textbf{Constraint filter:} Rows that exceed the $\varepsilon$ budget (with a 2\% tolerance), the per-round or total bandwidth, the number of rounds, or the MIA bound, that do not protect the requested unit (a row protecting a packet does not satisfy a stream or capture requirement), or whose rare-class recall is below the floor, are removed. Missing cost figures do not exclude a row; they are reported as not available.
    \item \textbf{Pareto front:} Among the remaining rows, configuration A dominates B if A is at least as good on every axis and better on at least one. The axes are the three utility measures (TSTR macro-F1, binary F1, rare-class recall), $\varepsilon$ under the assumed trust, the presence of secure aggregation, MB per round, and total MB. A utility difference counts only if it exceeds the seed standard deviation of both configurations; $\varepsilon$ values within 2\% and cost values within 5\% are treated as equal; a configuration with secure aggregation is preferred to one without when the other axes are equal.
    \item \textbf{Selection and ties:} The configuration with the best value on the requested priority is selected, and every configuration within the seed standard deviation of it is listed as tied.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Label Harmonisation and Rare Classes}\label{subsec:labels}
The labels are harmonised into a six-class mode: NORMAL, BCF, DELAYED, SYN, INVALID, and WILL. In this mode, the DoS and DDoS variants of the same attack family are merged into one class. The merge is supported by leakage check C5 (Section~\ref{subsec:leakage_checks}), which shows that DoS and DDoS variants of the same family are practically indistinguishable at packet level. The original eleven-class mode remains available in the pipeline but is used only for the robustness branch (Table~\ref{tab:robustness_branches}).

In this study, the rare classes are the attack classes whose training quota is at most 5,000 rows, namely DELAYED, SYN, INVALID, and WILL. Their rarity in training is a deliberate consequence of the quota design rather than a lack of raw data: as Table~\ref{tab:dataset_composition} shows, the raw dataset contains between 270,529 and 601,542 packets for each of them.

The quotas create an imbalance of 60:1 between the majority class (NORMAL, 60,000 rows) and the smallest class (WILL, 1,000 rows), which makes the evaluation of the generators demanding~\cite{he2009}. The validation and test splits, in contrast, are class-balanced (2,000 and 5,000 rows per class respectively), so that the evaluation measures are not dominated by the majority class.

\subsection{Schema Harmonisation and Feature Selection}\label{subsec:schema}
Preprocessing starts by harmonising the schema of the raw packet exports. Column names are converted to snake case. Wireshark text labels (such as \texttt{message\_type}, \texttt{qos\_level\_*}, and \texttt{requested\_qos}) are normalised to canonical codes, and boolean indicators (True/False, Set/Not set) are mapped to 0/1. Redundant columns with the suffix \texttt{\_1} are removed. For multi-valued cells that contain several MQTT messages, only the first element is kept, in line with the Normal export, which used the TShark option \texttt{occurrence=f}; the \texttt{n\_mqtt\_msgs} column is therefore dropped. Rows are then restricted to those whose protocol is TCP or MQTT, followed by a format audit of the time-based tokens.

To prevent leakage~\cite{kaufman2012, arp2022}, identifier and metadata columns are removed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{no} (row sequence), \texttt{epoch\_time} (absolute timestamp), and \texttt{time\_since\_reference\_\allowbreak or\_first\_frame} (which reveals the start time of the capture);
    \item \texttt{source}, because sender IP addresses would let a model memorise machine identities instead of learning traffic patterns;
    \item the free-text payload in \texttt{info}, and the \texttt{label} and \texttt{capture\_id} columns, which exist only in the Normal traffic.
\end{itemize}
\texttt{stream\_index} is kept only as grouping metadata and is not an input feature. The raw dataset contains no MAC address or port columns. After the feature selection decisions, the final schema has $D = 60$ input columns (Table~\ref{tab:feature_schema}).

\subsection{Group-Based Split and Quota Sampling}\label{subsec:split}
The eleven sub-classes (NORMAL and the DoS and DDoS variants of the five attack families) are split by group: whole capture groups are assigned to the training, validation, or test split according to a fixed \texttt{split\_seed}. This keeps highly correlated packets of the same TCP stream or capture session from appearing in more than one split~\cite{arp2022}.

The source data limits this approach: 8 of the 11 sub-classes come from a single capture file. For these sub-classes, the validation and test rows are taken as consecutive blocks of the same capture used for training (block split), separated by buffers of 1,000 rows to reduce adjacency leakage. TCP streams that straddle a block boundary are dropped entirely. This removes between 1.2\% and 9.9\% of the eligible rows of each affected sub-class and biases the sample slightly toward shorter connections.

After the split, rows are drawn at random without replacement according to the class quotas in Table~\ref{tab:quota}. The training quotas impose a strong imbalance, whereas the validation and test quotas are balanced.

\subsection{Preprocessing and Encoding}\label{subsec:preprocessing}
The preprocessing pipeline is fitted on the training split only; the validation and test splits are transformed with the stored parameters. Missing or non-applicable numeric values are imputed with 0, and an indicator column \texttt{\_is\_na} is added for every feature whose missing rate in the training split lies strictly between 0.005 and 0.995.

Numeric features with a skewness greater than 2 are transformed with $\log(1+x)$; time-delta features are first multiplied by 1,000 (seconds to milliseconds). Numeric columns are then standardised to zero mean and unit variance and clipped at $\pm 5\sigma$ to limit the influence of extreme values. Categorical features are one-hot encoded. To bound the dimension, only the ten most frequent training tokens of each categorical column are kept; other valid values are grouped into an \texttt{OTHER} token and missing values are mapped to a \texttt{NONE} token.

The normalisation statistics, category lists, and imputation parameters are fitted centrally on the pooled training split. This is a limitation of the method: it assumes a centralised view of the data before federation, and these statistics lie outside the privacy budget $\varepsilon$ (Section~\ref{subsec:threat_model}).

Every experiment is run with seeds $\{0, 1, 2\}$. \subsubsection*{Measures Supporting Reliability and Reproducibility}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Central run ledger:} Each run appends one row to \texttt{results/runs.csv} with a configuration hash and the Git commit of the code that produced it. The environment record of the reference run shows commit \texttt{9ee2a85e} and configuration hash \texttt{8c86c0b143d4}.
    \item \textbf{Checkpoint and resume:} Long training jobs save state; re-running a command compares against the ledger and skips completed configurations.
    \item \textbf{Automated reporting:} The tables, intervals, and figures of the final report are generated by code (\texttt{ppfeddata aggregate}, \texttt{ppfeddata interpret}); no result is typed by hand. A check recomputes macro-F1 from the saved prediction files and compares it with the ledger.
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility bundle:} \texttt{results/repro/} holds the exact package list (\texttt{pip\_freeze.txt}, identical to \texttt{requirements-lock.txt}), the configuration files, the environment record, and a manifest with SHA-256 digests of the ledger, the split and schema manifests, and the report.
    \item \textbf{Acceptance command:} \texttt{python -m ppfeddata.cli accept} checks the repository against the Definition of Done and writes a checklist of twelve items (D1--D10, with D6 split into three). This command does not run the test suite; the suite is run separately.
    \item \textbf{Clean-copy re-execution:} Phases 1--5 (inventory, harmonisation, sampling, preprocessing, leakage check) were re-run from the raw data in a clean copy of the repository with a fresh virtual environment, and the processed train, validation, and test arrays were compared with those of the main checkout. Phases 6--14 were not re-run in the clean copy.
    \item \textbf{Controlled trial:} A trial run of all ten configurations of the base matrix with seed 0 was compared row by row with the ledger of the main checkout (\texttt{SPEC\_DEVIATIONS.md}, 11.6).
    \item \textbf{Leakage checks:} The data pipeline is guarded by five checks (C1--C5, Section~3.2.6), and the real test split is verified against a stored hash.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations of the Methodology}\label{sec:methodology_limitations}
The limitations below arise from the design of the study and the scope of the techniques. The full list is maintained in \texttt{limitations.py}, which feeds the final report and the README; this section groups them. None of the statements below reports a result.

\subsubsection*{Data and Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Only one dataset is used; the study offers no evidence about other traffic or networks.
    \item The Normal traffic was re-exported from the original PCAP files and also contains the packets sent by the broker, which the authors' export settings exclude (Section~\ref{subsec:dataset}).
    \item Records are single packets, and the generators produce packets independently. Session-level temporal structure is therefore not reproduced, and the time-based features depend on how the captures were exported.
    \item Eight of the eleven sub-classes originate from a single capture file, so their validation and test rows are consecutive blocks of the same capture and not other captures. Removing TCP streams that straddle two blocks (\texttt{drop\_cross\_block\_streams}) reduces leakage between splits but biases the sample toward short connections; the share removed per sub-class is documented in the split manifest.
    \item The pipeline keeps only rows with protocol TCP or MQTT, keeps the first element of multi-valued cells, clips the tail of the time-gap feature at $5\sigma$, and treats nearly empty columns with the statistics of the rows where they apply plus an indicator.
    \item DoS and DDoS are merged in the six-class mode used for the experiment matrix.
    \item The validation and test splits are class-balanced (5,000 test rows per class) and do not follow the class proportions of real traffic.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Privacy Scope}
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\varepsilon$ is stated at record level (one packet) by default. Stream- and capture-level protection is optional for FedDP-Marginal and requires each unit to be held by one client and capped at $m$ rows. Under record-level $\varepsilon$, an attack session or capture is protected only through the weaker group bound~\cite{dwork2014}.
    \item Class labels, class counts, and client sizes are not protected (Section~\ref{subsec:threat_model}).
    \item Normalisation statistics are computed centrally on the pooled training split, as if each client had shared its data once. A real federation would have to compute them securely or fix them beforehand.
    \item Hyper-parameter selection on the real validation split is not private and lies outside $\varepsilon$.
    \item Distributed DP assumes that enough clients add their noise share; the guarantee degrades as the number of honest clients falls, which is why $\varepsilon$ is reported as a function of $h$.
    \item Accounting differs between variants: the Skellam variant uses the RDP bound of Agarwal et~al.~\cite{agarwal2021} and the RDP-to-$(\varepsilon, \delta)$ conversion of Opacus~\cite{yousefpour2021}, while the Gaussian variant uses zCDP~\cite{bun2016} with a looser conversion, so part of the difference between the two at the same $\varepsilon$ comes from the conversion and not from the mechanism.
    \item Malicious gateways (poisoning, Byzantine behaviour) are outside the threat model.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Generators}
\begin{itemize}
    \item FedDP-Marginal represents dependencies with a Chow--Liu tree~\cite{chow1968}, so each attribute depends on one parent; a second-order structure (MGb)~\cite{zhang2017privbayes} is only an option.
    \item Only one CVAE architecture is studied, and its hyper-parameter search used one seed per trial.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Evaluation and Statistics}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Three seeds are used for every configuration. Federated training can be sensitive to very small numerical perturbations, so a standard deviation over three seeds is a rough estimate.
    \item The bootstrap intervals and decision rules reflect the sampling of test rows (or whole streams) and of training seeds; they do not cover the choice of capture groups, the hyper-parameters, or the data sampling. Sensitivity A4 explores only two alternative choices of validation and test groups.
    \item The decision thresholds (for example the membership-inference AUC bound of 0.55, the $\varepsilon$ bound of 5, and the overhead ratio of 3) are heuristics fixed by the research team and not derived from data.
    \item Each membership-inference family tests one attack at record level and does not test group-level inference; each attack must pass a positive control to be informative.
    \item The decision rule does not use $p$-values or a multiple-comparison correction.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Execution Platform}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Federated learning is simulated on one CPU machine with Ray actors; no network latency, packet loss, or constrained-device behaviour is measured, and the reported times per round are compute times on this machine.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Baselines Not Compared and Parts Not Run}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Not compared:} FLAIM and AIM (the Private-PGM dependency was not installed), DP-CTGAN or other deep generators in the federation, federated SMOTE, and a directly trained federated classifier with distributed DP.
    \item \textbf{Written but not run after the change of primary generator:} M3f (distributed DP-FedSGD for the CVAE) and the SecAgg$^+$ quantisation-width sweep for the CVAE. Robustness branches A2 and A3 have configuration files but no runner.
\end{itemize}
